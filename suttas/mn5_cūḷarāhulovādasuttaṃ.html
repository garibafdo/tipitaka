416. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho bhagavato rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – ‘‘paripakkā kho rāhulassa vimuttiparipācanīyā dhammā. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ rāhulaṃ uttariṃ āsavānaṃ khaye vineyya’’nti. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘gaṇhāhi, rāhula, nisīdanaṃ; yena andhavanaṃ tenupasaṅkamissāma divāvihārāyā’’ti . ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato paṭissutvā nisīdanaṃ ādāya bhagavantaṃ piṭṭhito piṭṭhito anubandhi. Tena kho pana samayena anekāni devatāsahassāni bhagavantaṃ anubandhāni honti – ‘‘ajja bhagavā āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ uttariṃ āsavānaṃ khaye vinessatī’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā andhavanaṃ ajjhogāhetvā aññatarasmiṃ rukkhamūle paññatte āsane nisīdi. Āyasmāpi kho rāhulo bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ bhagavā etadavoca –
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Wood, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then, when the Blessed One was alone and secluded, this thought arose in his mind: “The qualities conducive to the liberation (vimutti) of Rāhula are ripe. Suppose I were to further train Rāhula in the destruction of the taints (āsava).”

Then, early in the morning, the Blessed One, having dressed and taking his bowl and robe, went into Sāvatthī for alms. After wandering for alms in Sāvatthī, after the meal, returning from his alms round, he addressed the venerable Rāhula, “Take, Rāhula, a sitting cloth; we will go to the Blind Men’s Grove (andhavana) for the day’s abiding.”

“So be it, Lord,” replied the venerable Rāhula. And taking a mat, he followed behind the Blessed One, step by step.

Then on that occasion, many thousands of deities followed the Blessed One, thinking: “Today the Blessed One will lead the venerable Rāhula further to the destruction of the taints (āsava)."

Then the Blessed One went into the Blind Men’s Grove (andhavana) and sat down at the root of a certain tree on a seat made ready. The venerable Rāhula also paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

When the venerable Rāhula was sitting to one side, the Blessed One said to him:
416.Evaṃme sutanti rāhulovādasuttaṃ. Tattha vimuttiparipācanīyāti vimuttiṃ paripācentīti vimuttiparipācanīyā. Dhammāti pannarasa dhammā. Te saddhindriyādīnaṃ visuddhikāraṇavasena veditabbā. Vuttañhetaṃ – ‘‘Assaddhe puggale parivajjayato, saddhe puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato pasādanīye suttante paccavekkhato imehi tīhākārehi saddhindriyaṃ visujjhati. Kusīte puggale parivajjayato āraddhavīriye puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato sammappadhāne paccavekkhato imehi tīhākārehi vīriyindriyaṃ visujjhati. Muṭṭhassatī puggale parivajjayato upaṭṭhitassatī puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato satipaṭṭhāne paccavekkhato imehi tīhākārehi satindriyaṃ visujjhati . Asamāhite puggale parivajjayato samāhite puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato jhānavimokkhe paccavekkhato imehi tīhākārehi samādhindriyaṃ visujjhati. Duppaññe puggale parivajjayato paññavante puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato gambhīrañāṇacariyaṃ paccavekkhato imehi tīhākārehi paññindriyaṃ visujjhati. Iti ime pañca puggale parivajjayato pañca puggale sevato bhajato payirupāsato pañca suttantakkhandhe paccavekkhato imehi pannarasahi ākārehi imāni pañcindriyāni visujjhantī’’ti (paṭi. ma. 1.185). Aparepi pannarasa dhammā vimuttiparipācanīyā – saddhādīni pañcimāni indriyāni, aniccasaññā, anicce dukkhasaññā, dukkhe anattasaññā, pahānasaññā, virāgasaññāti, imā pañca nibbedhabhāgiyā saññā, meghiyattherassa kathitā kalyāṇamittatādayo pañcadhammāti. Kāya pana velāya bhagavato etadahosīti. Paccūsasamaye lokaṃ volokentassa.
416. Thus have I heard: the Rāhulovāda Sutta.

There, vimuttiparipācanīyā means that which ripens liberation; therefore, vimuttiparipācanīyā.

Dhammā means fifteen things.

These should be understood in terms of being the cause of purification of the faith faculty, and so forth.

Thus it was said:

"By avoiding a person without faith, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon a person with faith, by reflecting on inspiring discourses, the faith faculty is purified by these three aspects.

By avoiding a lazy person, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon a person who has aroused energy, by reflecting on right effort, the energy faculty is purified by these three aspects.

By avoiding a person with a confused mind, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon a person with an established mind, by reflecting on mindfulness meditation, the mindfulness faculty is purified by these three aspects.

By avoiding a person who is not concentrated, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon a concentrated person, by reflecting on absorption and liberation, the concentration faculty is purified by these three aspects.

By avoiding a person who is unwise, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon a wise person, by reflecting on the practice of profound knowledge, the wisdom faculty is purified by these three aspects.

Thus, by avoiding these five types of person, by associating with, honoring, and attending upon these five types of person, by reflecting on the five aggregates of discourses, by these fifteen aspects these five faculties are purified" (Paṭi. Ma. 1. 185).

Moreover, there are fifteen things that ripen liberation: these five faculties beginning with faith, the perception of impermanence (aniccasaññā), the perception of suffering in impermanence (anicce dukkhasaññā), the perception of non-self in suffering (dukkhe anattasaññā), the perception of abandonment (pahānasaññā), the perception of detachment (virāgasaññā); these five perceptions pertaining to penetration (nibbedhabhāgiyā saññā), and the five qualities such as good friendship spoken by the Elder Meghiya.

But at what periods did the Blessed One speak thus?

Towards the time of dawn he was looking around the world.

417. “Rahula, what do you think? Is the eye permanent (nityaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?” “Impermanent, venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), (is) that unpleasant (duḥkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant (aniccaṃ), stressful (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) as: ‘This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am’?” “No, venerable sir (bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Are forms (rūpā) permanent (niccā) or impermanent (aniccā)?” “Impermanent, venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), (is) that unpleasant (duḥkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant (aniccaṃ), stressful (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) as: ‘This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am’?” “No, venerable sir (bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Is eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) permanent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?” “Impermanent, venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), (is) that unpleasant (duḥkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant (aniccaṃ), stressful (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) as: ‘This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am’?” “No, venerable sir (bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Is eye-contact (cakkhusamphasso) permanent (nicco) or impermanent (anicco)?” “Impermanent, venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), (is) that unpleasant (dukkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant (aniccaṃ), stressful (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) as: ‘This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am’?” “No, venerable sir (bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Are any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with eye-contact (cakkhusamphassa) as condition per-manent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?” “Impermanent, venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), (is) that unpleasant (dukkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant (aniccaṃ), stressful (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) as: ‘This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am’?” “No, venerable sir (bhante).”

419. ‘‘Evaṃ passaṃ, rāhula, sutavā ariyasāvako cakkhusmiṃ cakkhusmimpi (syā. kaṃ.) evamitaresupi nibbindati, rūpesu nibbindati, cakkhuviññāṇe nibbindati, cakkhusamphasse nibbindati, yamidaṃ cakkhusamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedanāgataṃ saññāgataṃ saṅkhāragataṃ viññāṇagataṃ tasmimpi nibbindati. Sotasmiṃ nibbindati, saddesu nibbindati…pe… , ghānasmiṃ nibbindati, gandhesu nibbindati… jivhāya nibbindati, rasesu nibbindati… kāyasmiṃ nibbindati, phoṭṭhabbesu nibbindati… manasmiṃ nibbindati, dhammesu nibbindati, manoviññāṇe nibbindati, manosamphasse nibbindati, yamidaṃ manosamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedanāgataṃ saññāgataṃ saṅkhāragataṃ viññāṇagataṃ tasmimpi nibbindati. Nibbindaṃ virajjati, virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Imasmiñca pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne āyasmato rāhulassa anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimucci. Tāsañca anekānaṃ devatāsahassānaṃ virajaṃ vītamalaṃ dhammacakkhuṃ udapādi – ‘yaṃ kiñci samudayadhammaṃ sabbaṃ taṃ nirodhadhamma’’nti. Cūḷarāhulovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ pañcamaṃ.
419. Seeing thus, Rāhula, a well-taught noble disciple becomes disenchanted with the eye (cakṣusmiṃ)... he becomes disenchanted with forms (rūpesu), disenchanted with eye-consciousness (cakṣuviññāṇe), disenchanted with eye-contact (cakṣusamphassa), and disenchanted with any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with eye-contact as condition. He becomes disenchanted with that as well.

He becomes disenchanted with the ear (sotasmiṃ), he becomes disenchanted with sounds (saddesu) ... He becomes disenchanted with the nose (ghānasmiṃ), he becomes disenchanted with odors (gandhesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the tongue (jivhāya), he becomes disenchanted with tastes (rasesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the body (kāyasmiṃ), he becomes disenchanted with tangibles (phoṭṭhabbesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the mind (manasmiṃ), he becomes disenchanted with mind-objects (dhammesu), he becomes disenchanted with mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇe), he becomes disenchanted with mind-contact (manosamphassa), and he becomes disenchanted with any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with mind-contact as condition. He becomes disenchanted with that as well.

Being disenchanted (nibbindaṃ), he becomes dispassionate (virajjati); through dispassion (virāgā) he is liberated (vimuccati). With release (vimuttasmiṃ), there is the knowledge (ñāṇaṃ) "It is liberated (vimuttamiti)."

‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā)’ he understands.

The Blessed One spoke thus (idamavoca bhagavā). The venerable Rāhula was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words (attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti). Now while this discourse was being spoken, through non-clinging (anupādāya) the venerable Rāhula’s mind (cittaṃ) was liberated (vimucci) from the taints (āsavehi). And in those many thousands of deities (devatāsahassānaṃ) there arose the stainless (virajaṃ), immaculate (vītamalaṃ) vision of the Dhamma (dhammacakkhuṃ): “All that is subject to arising (samudayadhammaṃ), is all subject to cessation (nirodhadhammaṃ).”

The Cūḷarāhulovāda Sutta, the fifth, is finished (cūḷarāhulovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ pañcamaṃ).
419.Anekānaṃ devatāsahassānanti āyasmatā rāhulena padumuttarassa bhagavato pādamūle pālitanāgarājakāle patthanaṃ paṭṭhapentena saddhiṃ patthanaṃ paṭṭhapitadevatāyeva. Tāsu pana kāci bhūmaṭṭhakā devatā, kāci antalikkhakā, kāci cātumahārājikā, kāci devaloke, kāci brahmaloke nibbattā. Imasmiṃ pana divase sabbā ekaṭṭhāne andhavanasmiṃyeva sannipatitā. Dhammacakkhunti upāliovāda- (ma. ni. 2.69) dīghanakhasuttesu (ma. ni. 2.206) paṭhamamaggo dhammacakkhunti vutto, brahmāyusutte (ma. ni. 2.395) tīṇi phalāni, imasmiṃ sutte cattāro maggā, cattāri ca phalāni dhammacakkhunti veditabbāni. Tattha hi kāci devatā sotāpannā ahesuṃ, kāci sakadāgāmī, anāgāmī, khīṇāsavā. Tāsañca pana devatānaṃ ettakāti gaṇanavasena paricchedo natthi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Rāhulovādasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
419. Āyasmā Rāhula made the following prayer at the foot of the Blessed Padumuttara, during the time of Pālitanāga, the king, saying, "Of the thousands of gods (anekānaṃ devatāsahassāna)," and he established the prayer together with the established deity.

Moreover, some of those deities were earth-dwelling (bhūmaṭṭhakā devatā), some were in the sky (antalikkhakā), some were of the realm of the Four Great Kings (cātumahārājikā), some were born in the heaven of the gods (devaloke), and some were born in the Brahma-world (brahmaloke nibbattā).

On this day, all of them gathered in one place, in the Blind Grove (andhavanasmiṃyeva sannipatitā).

Dhammacakkhu [is explained] in the Upāli instruction (M.N. 2.69), in the Dīghanakha Sutta (M.N. 2.206), the first path (paṭhamamaggo) is called dhammacakkhu, in the Brahmāyu Sutta (M.N. 2.395), there are three fruits (tīṇi phalāni), in this sutta, there are four paths (cattāro maggā), and four fruits (cattāri ca phalāni) should be understood as dhammacakkhu.

There, some deities were stream-enterers (sotāpannā ahesuṃ), some were once-returners (sakadāgāmī), non-returners (anāgāmī), [and] arahants with taints destroyed (khīṇāsavā).

And there is no determination of the number of those deities as being so many (ettakāti gaṇanavasena paricchedo natthi).

The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

The Rāhulovāda Sutta Commentary of the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Majjhima Nikāya, is finished.
