353. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena aññatarassa gahapatissa ekaputtako piyo manāpo kālaṅkato hoti. Tassa kālaṃkiriyāya neva kammantā paṭibhanti na bhattaṃ paṭibhāti. So āḷāhanaṃ gantvā kandati – ‘‘kahaṃ, ekaputtaka, kahaṃ, ekaputtakā’’ti! Atha kho so gahapati yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho taṃ gahapatiṃ bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘na kho te, gahapati, sake citte ṭhitassa indriyāni, atthi te indriyānaṃ aññathatta’’nti. ‘‘Kiñhi me, bhante, indriyānaṃ nāññathattaṃ bhavissati; mayhañhi, bhante, ekaputto piyo manāpo kālaṅkato. Tassa kālaṃkiriyāya neva kammantā paṭibhanti, na bhattaṃ paṭibhāti. Sohaṃ āḷāhanaṃ gantvā kandāmi – ‘kahaṃ, ekaputtaka, kahaṃ, ekaputtakā’’’ti! ‘‘Evametaṃ, gahapati, evametaṃ, gahapati evametaṃ gahapati (pī. sakideva), evameva (sī. sakideva)! Piyajātikā hi, gahapati, sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā piyappabhavikā’’ti. ‘‘Kassa kho kissa nu kho (sī.) nāmetaṃ, bhante, evaṃ bhavissati – ‘piyajātikā sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā piyappabhavikā’ti? Piyajātikā hi kho, bhante, ānandasomanassā piyappabhavikā’’ti. Atha kho so gahapati bhagavato bhāsitaṃ anabhinanditvā paṭikkositvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkāmi.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthi in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s monastery.

Now on that occasion, a certain householder’s ( gahapati ) dear and beloved only son had died.

After his death (kālaṃkiriyāya), he had no more desire to work or to eat.

He went to the charnel ground (āḷāhanaṃ) and cried: “Where, my only child (ekaputtaka)? Where, my only child?” Then that householder approached the Blessed One; after approaching, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

The Blessed One said this to the householder who was sitting to one side: “Householder, your faculties are not those of one in control of his own mind. Your faculties are deranged (aññathattaṃ).”

“How could my faculties not be deranged, venerable sir (bhante)? For my dear and beloved only son has died.

After his death, he had no more desire to work or to eat.

I go to the charnel ground and cry: ‘Where, my only child, where, my only child?’” “That is so, householder, that is so, householder! For sorrow, lamentation, suffering, grief, and despair are born from what is beloved (piyappabhavikā).”

“But venerable sir, why should it be that joy and happiness (ānandasomanassā) are born from what is beloved?”

Then that householder, displeased and disapproving of the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat and left.
353.Evaṃme sutanti piyajātikasuttaṃ. Tattha neva kammantā paṭibhantīti na sabbena sabbaṃ paṭibhanti, pakatiniyāmena pana na paṭibhanti. Dutiyapadepi eseva nayo. Ettha ca na paṭibhātīti na ruccati. Āḷāhananti susānaṃ. Aññathattanti vivaṇṇatāya aññathābhāvo. Indriyāni nāma manoviññeyyā dhammā, patiṭṭhitokāsaṃ pana sandhāya idaṃ vuttaṃ. Piyajātikāti piyato jāyanti. Piyappabhāvikāti piyato pabhavanti.
Thus have I heard: the discourse on those born of affection.

There, neva kammantā paṭibhantī means they do not appear in every way in every respect, but they do not appear in the natural order.

And the same method applies to the second phrase as well.

And here, na paṭibhātī means it is not pleasing.

Āḷāhana means a cemetery (śmaśāna).

Aññathatta means alteration due to discoloration, an altered state.

Indriyāni (faculties) means mental cognizable things, but this is said with reference to an established opportunity.

Piyajātikā means they are born of affection (priya).

Piyappabhāvikā means they arise from affection (priya).

354. Now on that occasion some gamblers were playing with dice not far from the Blessed One.

Then the householder went to those gamblers and said: “Just now, sirs, I went to the śramaṇa Gotama; and after paying homage to the śramaṇa Gotama, I sat down at one side.

When I had done so, the śramaṇa Gotama said to me: Householder, your faculties (indriyāṇi) are not those of one in control of his own mind. Your faculties (indriyāṇi) are deranged."

When this was said, I said to the śramaṇa Gotama: "But, sir (bhante), why should my faculties (indriyāṇi) not be deranged? Sir (bhante), my only son, dear and appealing, is dead.

Since his death, I have no pleasure in work, nor does food taste good.

So I go to the charnel ground and cry: Where is my only son, where is my only son!‘ That’s how it is, householder, that’s how it is, householder! For sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair are born from those we love.’

‘But whose life, sir (bhante), would it not be like this – that sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair are born from those we love? For joy and happiness are born from those we love.’

Then, displeased with the words of the śramaṇa Gotama, disapproving of them, I rose from my seat and left."

“That’s how it is, householder, that’s how it is, householder! For joy and happiness are born from those we love."

Then the householder left thinking: “I agree with the gamblers.”

Eventually this story reached the king’s palace.

357. Piyā te vajirī [is said]. Thus, it occurred to him: "If I were to speak the introductory words, 'Formerly, O great king, in this very Sāvatthī, regarding a certain woman,' he might reject me, saying, 'Who did that to you? Go away, there is nothing here.' I will convince him with what is happening now," thinking thus, he said:

Vipariṇāmaññathābhāvā: Here, vipariṇāma is due to death, and aññathābhāva should be understood as due to going away, having fled with someone.

Vāsabhāyā: Vāsabhā is the name of a certain queen of the king; referring to her, he said.

Vāsabhāyā piyā te aha: Why did he say [this] at the very end? Thus, it occurred to him: "This king is angry with me. If I were to ask 'Are you fond of me?' at the very beginning, he might say, 'You are not dear to me, go away!' Thus, the story would not gain a footing." For the sake of the story gaining a footing, he asked at the very end.

Kāsikosalesu: Vipariṇāma is due to the state of being thrown away in the Kāsi and Kosala countries, and aññathābhāva should be understood as due to going into the hands of enemy kings.

Piyā te aha kāsikosalesu: Ācamehī: Give me water for rinsing [the mouth].

Having rinsed his mouth, washed his hands and feet, cleansed his face, and desiring to greet the Teacher, he spoke thus.

The rest is clear throughout.

Ācamehī.
