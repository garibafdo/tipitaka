154. How, O monks, when developed and cultivated, does mindfulness of the body become of great fruit and great benefit? Here, O monks, a monk, having gone to the forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty place, sits down, folds his legs crosswise, holds his body erect, and establishes mindfulness in front of him.

Ever mindful he breathes in, mindful he breathes out. Breathing in long, he understands: ‘I breathe in long’; or breathing out long, he understands: ‘I breathe out long.’ Breathing in short, he understands: ‘I breathe in short’; or breathing out short, he understands: ‘I breathe out short.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the whole body of breath’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the whole body of breath.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the bodily formation’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the bodily formation.’

As he abides thus diligent, ardent, and resolute, his memories and intentions based on the household life are abandoned.

With their abandoning, he steadies his mind right within, settles it, unifies it, and concentrates it.

That too is how a bhikkhu develops mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati).

Again, O monks, when walking, a monk understands: ‘I am walking’; when standing, he understands: ‘I am standing’; when sitting, he understands: ‘I am sitting’; when lying down, he understands: ‘I am lying down’; or he understands accordingly however his body is disposed.

As he abides thus diligent, ardent, and resolute, his memories and intentions based on the household life are abandoned.

With their abandoning, he steadies his mind right within, settles it, unifies it, and concentrates it.

That too is how a bhikkhu develops mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati).

Again, O monks, a bhikkhu is one who acts in full awareness when going forward and returning; who acts in full awareness when looking ahead and looking away; who acts in full awareness when flexing and extending his limbs; who acts in full awareness when wearing his robes and carrying his outer robe and bowl; who acts in full awareness when eating, drinking, consuming food, and tasting; who acts in full awareness when defecating or urinating; who acts in full awareness when walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent.

As he abides thus diligent, ardent, and resolute, his memories and intentions based on the household life are abandoned.

With their abandoning, he steadies his mind right within, settles it, unifies it, and concentrates it.

That too is how a bhikkhu develops mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati).

“Again, bhikkhus, quite secluded from sensual pleasures… a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna.
He suffuses, drenches, fills, and pervades this very body with the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, so that there is no part of his entire body unpervaded by the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion.

“Again, bhikkhus, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought… a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna.

As he abides thus diligent… that too is how a bhikkhu develops mindfulness of the body.

“Again, bhikkhus, with the fading away as well of rapture… he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna.

“Again, bhikkhus, with the abandoning of pleasure…he enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna.

He sits pervading this body with a pure bright mind, so that there is no part of his whole body unpervaded by the pure bright mind.

"antogadhāvāssā" means they are indeed contained within the cultivation of that bhikkhu.

"vijjābhāgiyā": Here, vijjābhāgiyā means associating with vijjā through connection. Vijjābhāgiyā also means existing in the division of vijjā, in the section of vijjā. There, vipassanāñāṇa (insight knowledge), manomayiddhi (mind-made power), and the six abhiññā (higher knowledges) are the eight vijjā. According to the first meaning, things associated with them are also vijjābhāgiyā. According to the latter meaning, any one vijjā is vijjā, the rest are vijjābhāgiyā. Thus, both vijjā and things associated with vijjā should be understood as vijjābhāgiyā.

Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, subhūmiyaṃ catumahāpathe ājaññaratho yutto assa ṭhito odhastapatodo obhastapatodo (ka.), ubhantarapaṭodo (syā. kaṃ.) ava + dhaṃsu + ta = odhasta-itipadavibhāgo; tamenaṃ dakkho yoggācariyo assadammasārathi abhiruhitvā vāmena hatthena rasmiyo gahetvā dakkhiṇena hatthena patodaṃ gahetvā yenicchakaṃ yadicchakaṃ sāreyyāpi paccāsāreyyāpi; evameva kho, bhikkhave, yassa kassaci kāyagatāsati bhāvitā bahulīkatā, so yassa yassa abhiññāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhammassa cittaṃ abhininnāmeti abhiññāsacchikiriyāya , tatra tatreva sakkhibhabbataṃ pāpuṇāti sati satiāyatane’’.
158. Bhikkhus, when anyone has developed and cultivated mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati), then when he inclines his mind towards realizing any state that may be realized by direct knowledge (abhijñāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhamma), he attains the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

It is just as, bhikkhus, if there were a water-vessel full of water, level to the brim, so that a crow could drink from it (kākapeyyo), placed on a stand (ādhāre ṭhapito). If a strong man were to tip it this way and that, water would come out.” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“So too, bhikkhus, when anyone has developed and cultivated mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati), then when he inclines his mind towards realizing any state that may be realized by direct knowledge (abhijñāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhamma), he attains the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

Suppose, bhikkhus, there were a square pond (pokkharaṇī) on level ground (same bhūmibhāge), surrounded by an embankment (āḷibandhā), full of water (udakassa pūrā) right up to the brim (samatittikā) so that crows could drink from it (kākapeyyā). If a strong man were to loosen the embankment (āḷiṃ muñceyya) this way and that, water would come out?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“So too, bhikkhus, when anyone has developed and cultivated mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati), then when he inclines his mind towards realizing any state that may be realized by direct knowledge (abhijñāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhamma), he attains the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

Suppose, bhikkhus, on even ground (subhūmiyaṃ) at a crossroads (catumahāpathe), there were a chariot (ājaññaratho) harnessed to thoroughbreds, waiting with goad (patoda) lying ready; a skilled trainer (dakkho yoggācariyo), a charioteer who can tame horses (assadammasārathi), could mount it, take the reins with his left hand and the goad with his right, and drive it forwards and backwards as he wished; so too, bhikkhus, when anyone has developed and cultivated mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati), then when he inclines his mind towards realizing any state that may be realized by direct knowledge (abhijñāsacchikaraṇīyassa dhamma), he attains the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

Sati sati āyataneti satisati kāraṇe. Kiṃ panettha kāraṇanti? Abhiññāva kāraṇaṃ. Āḷibandhāti mariyādabaddhā. Yānīkatāyāti yuttayānaṃ viya katāya. Vatthukatāyāti patiṭṭhākatāya. Anuṭṭhitāyāti anuppavattitāya. Paricitāyāti paricayakatāya. Susamāraddhāyāti suṭṭhu samāraddhāya susampaggahitāya. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Yānīkatāyā Vatthukatāyā Anuṭṭhitāyā Paricitāyā Susamāraddhāyā Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Kāyagatāsatisuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
158. Kākapeyyo means it should be drunk by sitting at the mouth of the vessel without bending the neck like a crow.

Abhiññāsacchikaraṇīyassā means that which should be realized by higher knowledge (abhijñā).

Sakkhibhabbataṃ pāpuṇāti means attains the state of being a witness (pratyakṣabhāvaṃ prāpuṇāti).

Sati sati āyatane means when there is a cause (kāraṇe).

What is the cause here? Higher knowledge (abhijñā) is the cause.

Āḷibandhā means bound by a boundary (maryādabaddhā).

Yānīkatāyā means like something made with a suitable vehicle (yuttayānaṃ viya katāya).

Vatthukatāyā means made a foundation (patiṭṭhākatāya).

Anuṭṭhitāyā means not started (anuppavattitāya).

Paricitāyā means familiarity (paricayakatāya).

Susamāraddhāyā means well begun, well undertaken (suṭṭhu samāraddhāya susampaggahitāya).

The rest is clear everywhere.

Aratiratisaho hoti, na ca taṃ arati sahati, uppannaṃ aratiṃ abhibhuyya viharati. ‘‘Bhayabheravasaho hoti, na ca taṃ bhayabheravaṃ sahati, uppannaṃ bhayabheravaṃ abhibhuyya viharati. ‘‘Khamo hoti sītassa uṇhassa jighacchāya pipāsāya ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīsapasamphassānaṃ duruttānaṃ durāgatānaṃ vacanapathānaṃ, uppannānaṃ sārīrikānaṃ vedanānaṃ dukkhānaṃ tibbānaṃ kharānaṃ kaṭukānaṃ asātānaṃ amanāpānaṃ pāṇaharānaṃ adhivāsakajātiko hoti. ‘‘Catunnaṃ jhānānaṃ ābhicetasikānaṃ diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārānaṃ nikāmalābhī hoti akicchalābhī akasiralābhī. ‘‘So anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccānubhoti. Ekopi hutvā bahudhā hoti, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hoti, āvibhāvaṃ…pe… yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vatteti. ‘‘Dibbāya sotadhātuyā visuddhāya atikkantamānusikāya ubho sadde suṇāti dibbe ca mānuse ca, ye dūre santike ca…pe…. ‘‘Parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ cetasā ceto paricca pajānāti. Sarāgaṃ vā cittaṃ ‘sarāgaṃ citta’nti pajānāti, vītarāgaṃ vā cittaṃ…pe… sadosaṃ vā cittaṃ… vītadosaṃ vā cittaṃ… samohaṃ vā cittaṃ… vītamohaṃ vā cittaṃ… saṃkhittaṃ vā cittaṃ… vikkhittaṃ vā cittaṃ… mahaggataṃ vā cittaṃ… amahaggataṃ vā cittaṃ… sauttaraṃ vā cittaṃ… anuttaraṃ vā cittaṃ… samāhitaṃ vā cittaṃ… asamāhitaṃ vā cittaṃ… vimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ… avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ ‘avimuttaṃ citta’nti pajānāti. ‘‘So anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo…pe… iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati. ‘‘Dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe, sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāti. ‘‘Āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati. ‘‘Kāyagatāya, bhikkhave, satiyā āsevitāya bhāvitāya bahulīkatāya yānīkatāya vatthukatāya anuṭṭhitāya paricitāya susamāraddhāya ime dasānisaṃsā pāṭikaṅkhā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Kāyagatāsatisuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.
159. “Bhikkhus, when mindfulness of the body (kāyagatā sati) has been cultivated, developed, practiced repeatedly, made one's vehicle, made one's foundation, established, consolidated, and well undertaken, these ten benefits may be expected.

One becomes a conqueror of discontent and delight (aratiratisaha), and discontent does not conquer oneself; one abides overcoming discontent whenever it arises.

“One becomes a conqueror of fear and dread (bhayabheravasaha), and fear and dread do not conquer oneself; one abides overcoming fear and dread whenever they arise.

“One bears cold and heat, hunger and thirst, and contact with gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, the sun, and creeping things; one endures ill-spoken, unwelcome words and arisen bodily feelings that are painful, racking, sharp, piercing, disagreeable, distressing, and menacing to life. One is by nature capable of endurance (adhivāsakajātiko).

“Obtaining at will, without trouble, and without difficulty, the four jhānas, which pertain to the mind, are a dwelling of ease in the present.

“One experiences various kinds of supernormal power (anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccānubhoti): Being one, one becomes many; being many, one becomes one; one wields bodily mastery even as far as the Brahma-world.

“With the divine ear element (dibbāya sotadhātuyā), which is purified and surpasses the human, one hears both kinds of sounds, the divine and the human, those that are far as well as near…

“One understands the minds of other beings and persons, encompassing them with one's own mind. One understands a mind with lust (sarāga) as ‘a mind with lust,’ a mind without lust (vītarāga)… a mind with aversion (sadosa)… a mind without aversion (vītadosa)… a mind with delusion (samoha)… a mind without delusion (vītamoha)… a constricted mind (saṃkhitta)… a distracted mind (vikkhitta)… an exalted mind (mahaggata)… an unexalted mind (amahaggata)… a surpassed mind (sauttara)… an unsurpassed mind (anuttara)… a concentrated mind (samāhita)… an unconcentrated mind (asamāhita)… a liberated mind (vimutta)… an unliberated mind (avimutta), ‘an unliberated mind.’

“One recollects one’s manifold past lives (anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati), i.e., one birth, two births… Thus with their aspects and particulars, one recollects one’s manifold past lives.

“With the divine eye (dibbena cakkhunā), which is purified and surpasses the human, one sees beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate, and one understands how beings pass on according to their actions.

“By realizing for oneself with direct knowledge, one here and now enters upon and abides in the deliverance of mind (cetovimutti) and deliverance by wisdom (paññāvimutti) that are taintless with the destruction of the taints (āsavānaṃ khayā).

“Bhikkhus, when mindfulness of the body (kāyagatā sati) has been cultivated, developed, practiced repeatedly, made one's vehicle, made one's foundation, established, consolidated, and well undertaken, these ten benefits may be expected.”

This is what the Blessed One said.

Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Kāyagatāsatisutta, the ninth, is finished.
