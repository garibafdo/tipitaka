133. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha, at Isigili mountain. There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks.” “Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Fortunate One. The Fortunate One said this:

“Do you see that mountain, Vebhāra, monks?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “There used to be another name, another designation, for that mountain Vebhāra, monks.”

“Do you see that mountain, Paṇḍava, monks?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “There used to be another name, another designation, for that mountain Paṇḍava, monks.”

“Do you see that mountain, Vepulla, monks?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “There used to be another name, another designation, for that mountain Vepulla, monks.”

“Do you see that mountain, Gijjhakūṭa, monks?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “There used to be another name, another designation, for that mountain Gijjhakūṭa, monks.”

“Do you see this mountain, Isigili, monks?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “There used to be this same name, this same designation, for this mountain Isigili, monks.”

“In the past, monks, five hundred pratyekabuddhas lived for a long time on this mountain, Isigili. They were seen entering into this mountain, but after entering were not seen again. When people saw this, they said, ‘This mountain swallows these seers.’ Thus the designation ‘Isigili, Isigili’ arose. I will declare to you the names of the pratyekabuddhas; I will recount the names of the pratyekabuddhas; I will teach the names of the pratyekabuddhas. Listen to that and attend closely, I will speak.” “As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Fortunate One. The Fortunate One said this:

133. It was thus that I heard. This is the Iṣigilisutta.

Herein, "there was a different designation" means that when the designation Iṣigili arose for Iṣigili, it was known [to be] vebhāra, not vebhāra; there was a different designation for it.

"A different explanation" means this is a synonym of the first word.

The same rule applies to the rest.

At that time, the Blessed One, in the late afternoon, arose from meditation, went out from the Perfume Chamber, and sat down surrounded by the community of monks in a place where five mountains were visible, and systematically described these five mountains.

Herein, the Blessed One does not need the mountains, but when these mountains are systematically spoken of, the Iṣigilibhāva of Iṣigili must be spoken of.

While that was being spoken of, the names of the five hundred sons of Padumavatī, and the aspirations of Padumavatī, must be spoken of. Therefore, the Blessed One systematically described these five mountains.

"Entering, they are seen; having entered, they are not seen" means, having wandered for alms in a comfortable place, and having done their meal duties, they come and, as if opening the twin great doors in a cetiyagabbha, split that mountain in two and, having entered within, built places for staying at night and places for staying during the day, and lived there. Therefore he said this:

"These are sages" means these are individual Buddha sages.

Entering, they are seen; having entered, they are not seen.

These are sages. When did they live there? In the past, when a Tathāgata had not arisen, near Vārāṇasī, in a certain village, a certain daughter of a good family, while guarding a field, gave a lotus flower, together with five hundred handfuls of parched grain, to an individual Buddha, and wished for five hundred sons.

At that very moment, five hundred hunters, having given sweet meat, wished, "May we be her sons."

She, having remained as long as her life lasted, was reborn in the world of the gods, and having passed away from there, she was miraculously reborn in a lotus womb.

A certain ascetic, having seen her, tended to her. As she wandered about, lotuses arose from the earth at every step.

A certain forester, having seen [this], announced [it] to the king of Vārāṇasī.

The king had her brought [to him] and made [her] his chief queen, and she conceived.

Mahāpaduma-kumāra lived in his mother's womb; the rest were born dependent on the filth of the womb.

Having reached adulthood, while playing in the pleasure garden at Padumassara, sitting on each lotus, establishing decay and death, they brought forth the knowledge of individual enlightenment (paccekabodhiñāṇa).

This was their declaration verse:

"A lotus born from the leaves and petals of a lotus, beautifully flowered, frequented by swarms of bees, having known [it] to be subject to impermanence, one should wander alone like a rhinoceros horn."

At that time, they lived there; then the designation Iṣigili arose for that mountain.
