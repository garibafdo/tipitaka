129. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘dhāretha no tumhe, bhikkhave, mayā desitāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanānī’’ti? Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā mālukyaputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ahaṃ kho, bhante, dhāremi bhagavatā desitāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanānī’’ti. ‘‘Yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, mālukyaputta, dhāresi mayā desitāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanānī’’ti? ‘‘Sakkāyadiṭṭhiṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ desitaṃ dhāremi; vicikicchaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ desitaṃ dhāremi; sīlabbataparāmāsaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ desitaṃ dhāremi; kāmacchandaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ desitaṃ dhāremi; byāpādaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ desitaṃ dhāremi. Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, dhāremi bhagavatā desitāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanānī’’ti. ‘‘Kassa kho nāma tvaṃ, mālukyaputta, imāni evaṃ pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni desitāni dhāresi? Nanu, mālukyaputta , aññatitthiyā paribbājakā iminā taruṇūpamena upārambhena upārambhissanti? Daharassa hi, mālukyaputta, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa sakkāyotipi na hoti, kuto panassa uppajjissati sakkāyadiṭṭhi? Anusetvevassa anuseti tvevassa (sī. pī.) sakkāyadiṭṭhānusayo. Daharassa hi, mālukyaputta, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa dhammātipi na hoti, kuto panassa uppajjissati dhammesu vicikicchā? Anusetvevassa vicikicchānusayo. Daharassa hi, mālukyaputta, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa sīlātipi na hoti, kuto panassa uppajjissati sīlesu sīlabbataparāmāso? Anusetvevassa sīlabbataparāmāsānusayo . Daharassa hi, mālukyaputta, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa kāmātipi na hoti, kuto panassa uppajjissati kāmesu kāmacchando? Anusetvevassa kāmarāgānusayo. Daharassa hi, mālukyaputta, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa sattātipi na hoti, kuto panassa uppajjissati sattesu byāpādo? Anusetvevassa byāpādānusayo. Nanu, mālukyaputta, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā iminā taruṇūpamena upārambhena upārambhissantī’’ti? Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘etassa, bhagavā, kālo, etassa, sugata, kālo yaṃ bhagavā pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni deseyya. Bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī’’ti. ‘‘Tena hānanda, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca – anuseti tvevassa (sī. pī.)
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in the Jetavana in Anathapindika’s park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this: “Monks, do you remember the five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni) as taught by me?”

When this was said, the venerable Mälunkyaputta replied: “Venerable sir, I remember the five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni) as taught by the Blessed One.”

“In what way, Mälunkyaputta, do you remember the five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni) as taught by me?”

“Venerable sir, I remember personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) as a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ) taught by the Blessed One. I remember doubt (vicikicchā) as a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ) taught by the Blessed One. I remember adherence to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāsaṃ) as a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ) taught by the Blessed One. I remember sensual desire (kāmacchandaṃ) as a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ) taught by the Blessed One. I remember ill will (byāpādaṃ) as a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ) taught by the Blessed One. In this way, venerable sir, I remember the five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni) as taught by the Blessed One.”

“Mälunkyaputta, to whom do you remember my having taught these five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni) in that way? Mälunkyaputta, will not other ascetics and wanderers ridicule you with this youthful simile? A young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion ‘personality,’ so how could personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) arise in him? Yet the underlying tendency to personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhānusayo) lies within him. A young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion ‘teachings,’ so how could doubt (vicikicchā) about teachings arise in him? Yet the underlying tendency to doubt (vicikicchānusayo) lies within him. A young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion ‘rules,’ so how could adherence to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāso) arise in him? Yet the underlying tendency to adherence to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāsānusayo) lies within him. A young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion ‘sensual pleasures,’ so how could sensual desire (kāmacchando) arise in him? Yet the underlying tendency to sensual passion (kāmarāgānusayo) lies within him. A young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion ‘beings,’ so how could ill will (byāpādo) towards beings arise in him? Yet the underlying tendency to ill will (byāpādānusayo) lies within him. Mälunkyaputta, will not other ascetics and wanderers ridicule you with this youthful simile?”

When this was said, the venerable Ananda said to the Blessed One: “Lord, it is time for that! Well-Farer, it is time for that! If the Lord would teach about the five lower fetters (pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni), the monks would listen and remember it.”

“Then listen, Ananda, and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“So be it, O Lord.” the venerable Ananda replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said:
129.Evaṃme sutanti mahāmālukyasuttaṃ. Tattha orambhāgiyānīti heṭṭhā koṭṭhāsikāni kāmabhave nibbattisaṃvattanikāni. Saṃyojanānīti bandhanāni. Kassakho nāmāti kassa devassa vā manussassa vā desitāni dhāresi, kiṃ tvameveko assosi, na añño kocīti? Anusetīti appahīnatāya anuseti. Anusayamāno saṃyojanaṃ nāma hoti. Ettha ca bhagavatā saṃyojanaṃ pucchitaṃ, therenapi saṃyojanameva byākataṃ. Evaṃ santepi tassa vāde bhagavatā doso āropito. So kasmāti ce? Therassa tathāladdhikattā. Ayañhi tassa laddhi ‘‘samudācārakkhaṇeyeva kilesehi saṃyutto nāma hoti, itarasmiṃ khaṇe asaṃyutto’’ti. Tenassa bhagavatā doso āropito. Athāyasmā ānando cintesi – ‘‘bhagavatā bhikkhusaṅghassa dhammaṃ desessāmīti attano dhammatāyeva ayaṃ dhammadesanā āraddhā, sā iminā apaṇḍitena bhikkhunā visaṃvāditā. Handāhaṃ bhagavantaṃ yācitvā bhikkhūnaṃ dhammaṃ desessāmī’’ti. So evamakāsi. Taṃ dassetuṃ ‘‘evaṃ vutte āyasmā ānando’’tiādi vuttaṃ. Tattha sakkāyadiṭṭhipariyuṭṭhitenāti sakkāyadiṭṭhiyā gahitena abhibhūtena. Sakkāyadiṭṭhiparetenāti sakkāyadiṭṭhiyā anugatena. Nissaraṇanti diṭṭhinissaraṇaṃ nāma nibbānaṃ, taṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Appaṭivinītāti avinoditā anīhaṭā. Orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojananti heṭṭhābhāgiyasaṃyojanaṃ nāma hoti. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo. Sukkapakkho uttānatthoyeva. ‘‘Sānusayā pahīyatī’’ti vacanato panettha ekacce ‘‘aññaṃ saṃyojanaṃ añño anusayo’’ti vadanti. ‘‘Yathā hi sabyañjanaṃ bhatta’’nti vutte bhattato aññaṃ byañjanaṃ hoti, evaṃ ‘‘sānusayā’’ti vacanato pariyuṭṭhānasakkāyadiṭṭhito aññena anusayena bhavitabbanti tesaṃ laddhi. Te ‘‘sasīsaṃ pārupitvā’’tiādīhi paṭikkhipitabbā. Na hi sīsato añño puriso atthi. Athāpi siyā – ‘‘yadi tadeva saṃyojanaṃ so anusayo, evaṃ sante bhagavatā therassa taruṇūpamo upārambho duāropito hotī’’ti. Na duāropito, kasmā? Evaṃladdhikattāti vitthāritametaṃ. Tasmā soyeva kileso bandhanaṭṭhena saṃyojanaṃ, appahīnaṭṭhena anusayoti imamatthaṃ sandhāya bhagavatā ‘‘sānusayā pahīyatī’’ti evaṃ vuttanti veditabbaṃ. sakkāyadiṭṭhipariyuṭṭhitenā Sakkāyadiṭṭhiparetenā Nissaraṇa Appaṭivinītā Orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojana
129. Thus I have heard: the Mahāmālukyasutta.

There, orambhāgiyāni means the lower, portion-belonging attachments, pertaining to arising in the desire realm. Saṃyojanāni means bindings. Kassa kho nāmā means to whom, to what god or human, were [these teachings] taught and preserved? Did only you hear it, and no one else? Anusetī means it clings because it has not been abandoned. One who clings is called a saṃyojana.

Here, the Blessed One asked about saṃyojana, and the Elder also explained saṃyojana. Even so, the Blessed One found fault with his view. Why is that? Because the Elder held such a view. For this is his view: "One is only connected with the defilements at the moment of habitual practice, and unconnected at other moments." Therefore, the Blessed One found fault with him.

Then Āyasmā Ānanda thought: "This teaching of the Dharma has been begun by the Blessed One according to his own nature, thinking 'I will teach the Dharma to the Saṅgha of monks,' but it has been contradicted by this unwise monk. I will ask the Blessed One to teach the Dharma to the monks." So he did. To show this, it is said, "When this was said, Āyasmā Ānanda," etc.

There, sakkāyadiṭṭhipariyuṭṭhitenā means seized and overcome by sakkāyadiṭṭhi [personality belief]. Sakkāyadiṭṭhiparetena means followed by sakkāyadiṭṭhi. Nissaraṇa means that nibbāna is the escape from [wrong] views; he does not understand it as it really is. Appaṭivinītā means not removed, not driven away. Orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojana means it is called a lower-portion-belonging saṃyojana. The same method applies to the other clauses as well. The bright side is easy to understand. Because it is said that "it is abandoned together with the anusaya," some here say, "One saṃyojana is different, and one anusaya is different." Just as when it is said, "rice with curry," the curry is different from the rice, so because of the saying "with anusaya", it must be that the anusaya is different from pariyuṭṭhāna-sakkāyadiṭṭhi [personality belief in manifestation]; this is their view. They should be rejected by [passages beginning] "covering the head," etc. For there is no person other than the head.

But even if it were so – "if that same thing is the saṃyojana and the anusaya, then in that case, the Blessed One's rebuke of the Elder would be wrongly applied" – it is not wrongly applied. Why? Because he held such a view; this has been explained in detail. Therefore, it should be understood that the Blessed One said "it is abandoned together with the anusaya", intending to convey this meaning: that same defilement is a saṃyojana in the sense of a binding, and an anusaya in the sense of not being abandoned.

Sakkāyadiṭṭhipariyuṭṭhitenā, sakkāyadiṭṭhiparetena, nissaraṇa, appaṭivinītā, orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojana.

130. ‘‘Idhānanda , assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto, sappurisānaṃ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto sakkāyadiṭṭhipariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati sakkāyadiṭṭhiparetena; uppannāya ca sakkāyadiṭṭhiyā nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tassa sā sakkāyadiṭṭhi thāmagatā appaṭivinītā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ. Vicikicchāpariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati vicikicchāparetena; uppannāya ca vicikicchāya nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tassa sā vicikicchā thāmagatā appaṭivinītā orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ. Sīlabbataparāmāsapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati sīlabbataparāmāsaparetena; uppannassa ca sīlabbataparāmāsassa nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tassa so sīlabbataparāmāso thāmagato appaṭivinīto orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ. Kāmarāgapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati kāmarāgaparetena ; uppannassa ca kāmarāgassa nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tassa so kāmarāgo thāmagato appaṭivinīto orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ. Byāpādapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati byāpādaparetena; uppannassa ca byāpādassa nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tassa so byāpādo thāmagato appaṭivinīto orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ.
“Here, Ānanda, an untaught ordinary person who has no regard for noble ones and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, who has no regard for true men and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, abides with a mind obsessed and enslaved by personality view (satkāyadṛṣṭi), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from the arisen personality view (satkāyadṛṣṭi); and when that personality view (satkāyadṛṣṭi) has become habitual and is uneradicated in him, it is a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ). He abides with a mind obsessed and enslaved by doubt (vicikicchā), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen doubt (vicikicchā); and when that doubt (vicikicchā) has become habitual and is uneradicated in him, it is a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ). He abides with a mind obsessed and enslaved by adherence to rules and observances (śīlavrataparāmāśa), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen adherence to rules and observances (śīlavrataparāmāśa); and when that adherence to rules and observances (śīlavrataparāmāśa) has become habitual and is uneradicated in him, it is a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ). He abides with a mind obsessed and enslaved by sensual lust (kāmarāga), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen sensual lust (kāmarāga); and when that sensual lust (kāmarāga) has become habitual and is uneradicated in him, it is a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ). He abides with a mind obsessed and enslaved by ill will (vyāpāda), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen ill will (vyāpāda); and when that ill will (vyāpāda) has become habitual and is uneradicated in him, it is a lower fetter (orambhāgiyaṃ saṃyojanaṃ).

“Indeed, Ānanda, a well-taught noble disciple (ariyasāvako) who has regard for noble ones (ariyānaṃ dassāvī) and is skilled in the Dhamma of the noble ones (ariyadhammassa kovido), who is well-disciplined in the Dhamma of the noble ones (ariyadhamme suvinīto), who has regard for true men (sappurisānaṃ dassāvī) and is skilled in the Dhamma of true men (sappurisadhammassa kovido), who is well-disciplined in the Dhamma of true men (sappurisadhamme suvinīto), does not abide with a mind obsessed by personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) nor enslaved by personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi); and he understands as it actually is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from the arisen personality view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi).
And personality view (sā sakkāyadiṭṭhi) together with the underlying tendency to it (sānusayā) is abandoned (pahīyati) in him.
He does not abide with a mind obsessed by doubt (vicikicchā) nor enslaved by doubt (vicikicchā); and he understands as it actually is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from arisen doubt (vicikicchāya).
And doubt (sā vicikicchā) together with the underlying tendency to it (sānusayā) is abandoned (pahīyati) in him.
He does not abide with a mind obsessed by clinging to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāsa) nor enslaved by clinging to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāsa); and he understands as it actually is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from arisen clinging to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāsassa).
And clinging to rules and observances (so sīlabbataparāmāso) together with the underlying tendency to it (sānusayo) is abandoned (pahīyati) in him.
He does not abide with a mind obsessed by sensual lust (kāmarāga) nor enslaved by sensual lust (kāmarāga); and he understands as it actually is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from arisen sensual lust (kāmarāgassa).
And sensual lust (so kāmarāgo) together with the underlying tendency to it (sānusayo) is abandoned (pahīyati) in him.
He does not abide with a mind obsessed by ill will (byāpāda) nor enslaved by ill will (byāpāda); and he understands as it actually is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from arisen ill will (byāpādassa).
And ill will (so byāpādo) together with the underlying tendency to it (sānusayo) is abandoned (pahīyati) in him.

132. ‘‘Yo, ānanda, maggo yā paṭipadā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ pahānāya taṃ maggaṃ taṃ paṭipadaṃ anāgamma pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni ñassati vā dakkhati vā pajahissati vāti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato tacaṃ acchetvā phegguṃ acchetvā sāracchedo bhavissatīti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati; evameva kho, ānanda, yo maggo yā paṭipadā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ pahānāya taṃ maggaṃ taṃ paṭipadaṃ anāgamma pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni ñassati vā dakkhati vā pajahissati vāti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. ‘‘Yo ca kho, ānanda, maggo yā paṭipadā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ pahānāya taṃ maggaṃ taṃ paṭipadaṃ āgamma pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni ñassati vā dakkhati vā pajahissati vāti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato tacaṃ chetvā phegguṃ chetvā sāracchedo bhavissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati; evameva kho, ānanda, yo maggo yā paṭipadā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ pahānāya taṃ maggaṃ taṃ paṭipadaṃ āgamma pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni ñassati vā dakkhati vā pajahissati vāti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. “There is a path, Ānanda, a way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters; that anyone, without coming to that path, to that way, shall know or see or abandon the five lower fetters - this is not possible.

Just as when there is a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, it is not possible that anyone shall cut out its heartwood without cutting through its bark and sapwood, so too, there is a path …this is not possible.

“There is a path, Ānanda, a way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters; that someone, by coming to that path, to that way, shall know and see and abandon the five lower fetters - this is possible.

Just as, when there is a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, it is possible that someone shall cut Out its heartwood by cutting through its bark and sapwood, so too, there is a path… this is possible.

“Suppose, Ānanda, the river Gaṅgā were full of water right up to the brim so that crows could drink from it.

and then a feeble man came thinking: ‘By swimming across the stream with my arms, I shall get safely across to the further shore of this river Gaṅgā’; yet he would not be able to get safely across.

So too, when the dharma is being taught to someone for the cessation of personality (satkāya-nirodha), if his mind does not enter into it and acquire confidence, steadiness, and decision, then he can be regarded as like the strong man.

“Suppose, Ānanda, the river Gaṅgā were full of water right up to the brim so that crows could drink from it.

and then a strong man came thinking: ‘By swimming across the stream with my arms, I shall get safely across to the further shore of this river Gaṅgā’; and he would be able to get safely across.

So too, when the dharma is being taught to someone for the cessation of personality (satkāya-nirodha), if his mind enters into it and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision, then he can be regarded as like the strong man.

In " tacaṃ chetvā tiādīsu," this is a connection made through simile: the attainment (samāpatti) should be seen like cutting the bark (tacacchedo), insight (vipassanā) is like cutting the sapwood (pheggucchedo), the path (maggo) is like cutting the heartwood (sāracchedo). However, the practice (paṭipadā) is suitable as a mixture of mundane and supramundane. " evamete daṭṭhabbā ti," such individuals should be seen in this way.

133. "And what, Ānanda, is the path, what is the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters (orambhāgiya-saṃyojana)? Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu, quite secluded from sense-desires, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by thought and examination, born of seclusion, filled with rapture and joy, having abandoned attachment to the acquisitions, abandoning unwholesome dharmas, and with the stilling of all bodily distress.

Whatever exists therein of material form (rūpa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saṃjñā), formations (saṃskāra), and consciousness (vijñāṇa), he sees those states as impermanent, as suffering, as a disease, as a tumour, as a barb, as a calamity, as an affliction, as alien, as disintegrating, as void, as not self.

He turns his mind away from those states.

He turns his mind away from those states and directs it towards the deathless element thus: ‘This is the peaceful, this is the sublime, that is, the stilling of all formations, the relinquishing of all acquisitions, the destruction of craving (taṇhā), dispassion (virāga), cessation (nirodha), Nirvāṇa.’

Standing upon that, he attains the destruction of the taints (āsava). But if he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then because of that desire for the Dharma, that delight in the Dharma, with the destruction of the five lower fetters he becomes one due to reappear spontaneously, and there attain final Nirvāṇa without ever returning from that world.

This is the path, Ānanda, the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters.

“Again, Ānanda, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought…he enters upon and abides in the second jhāna… the third jhāna… the fourth jhāna.

Whatever exists therein of material form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness… he does not return from that world.

This is the path, Ānanda, the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters.

“Again, Ānanda, with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, with non-attention to perceptions of diversity, aware that ‘space is infinite,’ a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana).

Whatever exists therein of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness… he does not return from that world.

This is the path, Ānanda, the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters.

“Again, Ānanda, by completely surmounting the base of infinite space, aware that ‘consciousness is infinite,’ a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana).

Whatever exists therein of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness… he does not return from that world.

This is the path, Ānanda, the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters.

“Again, Ānanda, by completely surmounting the base of infinite consciousness, aware that ‘there is nothing,’ a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana).

Whatever exists therein of feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness… he does not return from that world.

This is the path, Ānanda, the way to the abandoning of the five lower fetters.”

133. Upadhivivekā means by discrimination of the basis.

By this, discrimination of the five strands of sense desire is spoken of.

Akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānā means by the abandonment of unwholesome factors, by this, abandonment of the hindrances is spoken of.

Kāyaduṭṭhullānaṃ paṭippassaddhiyā means by tranquilization of bodily unruliness, by this, tranquilization of bodily sloth and torpor is spoken of.

Vivicceva kāmehī means having become without desires by discrimination of the basis.

Vivicca akusalehī means having become without unwholesome [factors] by the abandonment of unwholesome factors and by tranquilization of bodily unruliness.

Yadeva tattha hotī means whatever exists there, that is, whatever phenomena ( rūpādi-dhamma-jātaṃ) exist there at the very moment of inner attainment (antosaṃāpatti), and which arise together with the attainment.

Te dhamme means those factors, that is, the factors like form, etc., which are spoken of in the manner beginning with "they have gone to form".

Aniccato means not permanent.

Dukkhato means not pleasurable.

Rogato etc. means in the case of diseases, rogato is due to the condition of being afflicted, gaṇḍato is due to the condition of internal affliction, sallato is due to the condition of not being rejected and the condition of producing suffering, aghato is due to the condition of suffering, ābādhato is due to the condition of disease, parato is due to the condition of being unsustained, palokato is due to the condition of perishing, suññato is due to the condition of being without substance, anattato is due to the condition of not being self.

There, by the two terms aniccatas and palokato, the characteristic of impermanence is spoken of; by the six, beginning with dukkhato, the characteristic of suffering; by the three, parato, suññato, anattato, the characteristic of not-self.

So tehi dhammehī means he, by those factors, that is, by the five aggregates (pañcakkhandha) as seen by superimposing the three characteristics (tilakkhaṇa) in this way in inner attainment (antosaṃāpatti).

Cittaṃ paṭivāpetī means he turns back the mind, withdraws it, releases it, removes it.

Upasaṃharatī means he leads [the mind] near; he leads the mind of insight near to the unconditioned (asaṅkhata) deathless (amata) element (dhātu), that is, nirvāṇa, by way of praise, by way of learning, by way of designation, [thinking,] "This is peace, this is nirvāṇa."

However, the path-mind does not speak thus, [saying,] "This is peace, this is excellent," merely through the force of making nirvāṇa its object. But in this way, while penetrating that, he leads the mind near there, is the meaning.

So tattha ṭhito means he stands in that insight (vipassanā) which has the three characteristics (tilakkhaṇa) as its object (ārammaṇa).

Āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇātī means he attains the destruction of the āsavas by developing the four paths in due order.

Teneva dhammarāgenā means by interest (chandarāga) in the states of serenity and insight (samatha-vipassanā-dhamma).

For one who, in those states of serenity and insight, is able to completely consume interest, attains arhatship; one who is unable to, becomes a non-returner (anāgāmī).

So, by those factors, he turns back the mind; he leads [the mind] near; he stands in that place; he attains the destruction of the āsavas; by that very interest in the Dharma; whatever there is there, sensation-gone-to-that-place, here, however, form is not taken.

Why? Because of having transcended [it].

For here, having attained to the rūpāvacara-jhāna below, having transcended form, he attains to the arūpāvacara-samāpatti. Thus, by way of serenity, form is transcended. Below, having properly experienced form, having transcended that, now he experiences the formless. Thus, by way of insight, form is transcended.

However, in the formless, since form does not exist at all, with reference to that also, form is not taken here.

Whatever sensation gone to that place exists there. Now what? He asks, "What shall I ask?" For one going by way of serenity, singleness of mind is the prominent feature; he is called mind-liberated.

For one going by way of insight, wisdom is the prominent feature; he is called wisdom-liberated. Here, there is no doubt for the elder.

This is just the nature of the phenomena. However, among those going by way of serenity, one is called mind-liberated, one is called wisdom-liberated.

Even among those going by way of insight, one is called wisdom-liberated, one is called mind-liberated. Here, he asks, what is the reason?

Now what? I will speak of the diversity of the faculties means I will speak of the difference of the faculties.

This has been said: "It is not you, Ānanda, who, having fulfilled the ten perfections, penetrated omniscience; therefore, this is not clear to you."

"But I penetrated [it]; therefore, this is clear to me."

Here, the diversity of the faculties is the reason.
Indeed, while going by way of tranquility, if a bhikkhu has one-pointedness of mind ( cittekaggatā) as his chief concern (dhuraṃ), he is called liberated in mind (cetovimutto).

If one has wisdom (paññā) as their chief concern, they are called liberated by wisdom (paññāvimutto).

And while going by way of insight (vipassanā), if one has wisdom (paññā) as their chief concern (dhuraṃ), he is called liberated by wisdom (paññāvimutto).

If one has one-pointedness of mind (cittekaggatā) as his chief concern, he is called liberated in mind (cetovimutto).

Two foremost disciples attained arahatta by means of tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā) as their chief concern (dhurena).

Among them, the general of the Dharma (dhammasenāpati) was born liberated by wisdom (paññāvimutto), and the great thera Moggallana (mahāmoggallānatthero) was liberated in mind (cetovimutto).

Thus, the difference in faculties (indriyavemattamettha) should be understood as the reason.

The rest is clear throughout.

I speak of the difference in faculties (indriyavemattataṃ vadāmī).
