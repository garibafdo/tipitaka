276. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā ānando upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandasseti samādapeti samuttejeti sampahaṃseti, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca bhāsati. Atha kho bhagavā sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yenupaṭṭhānasālā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Nisajja kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘ko nu kho, bhikkhave, upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti? ‘‘Āyasmā, bhante, ānando upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi , bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesiṃ samādapesiṃ samuttejesiṃ sampahaṃsesiṃ, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsiṃ – ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate muni’’.
Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jetavana (jetavane) at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Now on that occasion, the venerable Ānanda was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dhamma (dhammiyā kathāya) in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ). He was reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of "One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment" (bhaddekarattassa).

Then, in the evening (sāyanhasamayaṃ), the Blessed One (bhagavā) rose from meditation (paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito) and went to the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālā). On arrival, he sat down on a seat (āsane) made ready (paññatte).

Having sat down (nisajja), the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū) : "Monks (bhikkhave), who has been instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dhamma (dhammiyā kathāya) in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ)? Who has been reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of 'One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment' (bhaddekarattassa)?"

"[Venerable] Ānanda (ānando), venerable sir (bhante), has been instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dhamma (dhammiyā kathāya) in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ). He has been reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of 'One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment' (bhaddekarattassa)."

Then the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the venerable Ānanda (ānandaṃ): "Ānanda (ānanda), how were you instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dhamma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of 'One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment' (bhaddekarattassa)?"

"In this way (evaṃ kho), venerable sir (bhante), I have been instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dhamma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of 'One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment' (bhaddekarattassa):

One should not yearn for the past (atītaṃ nānvāgameyya), nor should one long for the future (nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ);

For the past is gone (yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ), and the future is yet unreached (appattañca anāgataṃ).

But whoever sees the present (paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ) in each and every condition (tattha tattha vipassati),

Unmoved (asaṃhīraṃ) and unshaken (asaṃkuppaṃ), let them cultivate that insight (taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye).

Earnestness (kiccamātappaṃ) should be done today (ajjeva), for who knows if death (maraṇaṃ) will come tomorrow (suve)?

There is no bargaining with Mortality (na hi no saṅgaraṃ), or his mighty horde (mahāsenena maccunā).

One who lives thus, zealous (evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ), unwearied day and night (ahorattamatanditaṃ),

That one truly is ‘one with a fortunate attachment’ (taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti), so the peaceful sage declares (santo ācikkhate muni)."
276.Evaṃme sutanti ānandabhaddekarattasuttaṃ. Tattha paṭisallānā vuṭṭhitoti phalasamāpattito vuṭṭhito. Ko nu kho, bhikkhaveti jānantova kathāsamuṭṭhāpanatthaṃ pucchi.
Thus have I heard. Ānanda Bhaddekaratta Sūtra.

There, "risen from seclusion" means risen from the fruition attainment (phalasamāpatti).

"What now, monks?" he asked, even though he knew, in order to initiate the discussion.

277. ‘‘Kathañca, āvuso, atītaṃ anvāgameti? Evaṃrūpo ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃvedano ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃsañño ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃsaṅkhāro ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃviññāṇo ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, āvuso, atītaṃ anvāgameti. ‘‘Kathañca, āvuso, atītaṃ nānvāgameti? Evaṃrūpo ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃvedano ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃsañño ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃsaṅkhāro ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃviññāṇo ahosiṃ atītamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, āvuso, atītaṃ nānvāgameti. ‘‘Kathañca, āvuso, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati? Evaṃrūpo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃvedano siyaṃ…pe… evaṃsañño siyaṃ… evaṃsaṅkhāro siyaṃ… evaṃviññāṇo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, āvuso, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati. ‘‘Kathañca, āvuso, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati? Evaṃrūpo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃvedano siyaṃ…pe… evaṃsañño siyaṃ… evaṃsaṅkhāro siyaṃ… evaṃviññāṇo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, āvuso, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati. ‘‘Kathañca, āvuso, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati? Idha, āvuso, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto sappurisānaṃ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto rūpaṃ attato samanupassati, rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, attani vā rūpaṃ, rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; vedanaṃ… saññaṃ… saṅkhāre… viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati, viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, attani vā viññāṇaṃ, viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ – evaṃ kho, āvuso, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati. ‘‘Kathañca , āvuso, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati? Idha, āvuso, sutavā ariyasāvako ariyānaṃ dassāvī ariyadhammassa kovido ariyadhamme suvinīto sappurisānaṃ dassāvī sappurisadhammassa kovido sappurisadhamme suvinīto na rūpaṃ attato samanupassati, na rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, na attani vā rūpaṃ, na rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; na vedanaṃ… na saññaṃ… na saṅkhāre… na viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati, na viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, na attani vā viññāṇaṃ, na viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ – evaṃ kho, āvuso, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati. ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesiṃ samādapesiṃ samuttejesiṃ sampahaṃsesiṃ, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsi’’nti.
277. “And how, friend, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past’…! had such perception in the past’…! had such formations in the past,’…! had such consciousness in the past,’ one finds delight in that. That is how, friend, one revives the past.

“And how, friend, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past’… one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such perception in the past’… one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such formations in the past’… one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such consciousness in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, friend, one does not revive the past.

“And how, friend, does one build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such material form in the future,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such feeling in the future,’… ‘I may have such perception in the future’…! may have such formations in the future’… ‘I may have such consciousness in the future,’ one finds delight in that. That is how, friend, one builds up hope upon the future.

“And how, friend, does one not build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such material form in the future,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such feeling in the future,’… ‘I may have such perception in the future’… ‘I may have such formations in the future’… ‘I may have such consciousness in the future,’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, friend, one does not build up hope upon the future.

“And how, friend, does one get caught up in present phenomena? Here, friend, an uninstructed ordinary person who has not seen the noble ones, is not versed in the teachings of the noble ones, is not trained in the teachings of the noble ones, has not seen the superior persons, is not versed in the teachings of the superior persons, is not trained in the teachings of the superior persons, regards form as self (ātman), or self as possessing form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He regards feeling… perception… formations… consciousness as self, or self as possessing consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That is how, friend, one gets caught up in present phenomena.

“And how, friend, does one not get caught up in present phenomena? Here, friend, an instructed noble disciple who has seen the noble ones, is versed in the teachings of the noble ones, is well-trained in the teachings of the noble ones, has seen the superior persons, is versed in the teachings of the superior persons, is well-trained in the teachings of the superior persons, does not regard form as self (ātman), or self as possessing form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He does not regard feeling… perception… formations… consciousness as self, or self as possessing consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That is how, friend, one does not get caught up in present phenomena.

“One should not revive the past, nor hope for the future;
What is past is relinquished, and the future is not yet attained.

“But whoever sees the present phenomenon in each and every place,
Unmoved, unangered, knowing it, let him cultivate it.

“Today, the effort must be made; who knows, death may come tomorrow?
For we have no bargain with Death and his great army.

“The silent sages declare that one who dwells thus, ardent, unwearied day and night, is truly ‘one who has one fortunate attachment.’”

“Thus, venerable sir, I was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the bhikkhus with talk on the Dhamma, and reciting the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’”

278. ‘‘Sādhu , sādhu, ānanda! Sādhu kho tvaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsi – ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya…pe… Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. ‘‘Kathañca, ānanda, atītaṃ anvāgameti…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, atītaṃ anvāgameti. Kathañca, ānanda, atītaṃ nānvāgameti…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, atītaṃ nānvāgameti. Kathañca, ānanda, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati. Kathañca, ānanda, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati. Kathañca, ānanda, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati. Kathañca, ānanda, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati…pe… evaṃ kho, ānanda, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati. ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya…pe… Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Ānandabhaddekarattasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dutiyaṃ.
278. "Well said, Ānanda! Well said, Ānanda. You have shown, encouraged, aroused, and delighted the monks with a discourse on the Dharma, and you have spoken of the summary and the analysis of the Fortunate One Night (bhaddekaratta)."

"He should not pursue the past…pe…

That one is truly fortunate, the sage declares, who is at peace."

"And how, Ānanda, does one revive the past?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, does one revive the past.

And how, Ānanda, does one not revive the past?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, does one not revive the past.

And how, Ānanda, does one build up hope upon the future?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, does one build up hope upon the future.

And how, Ānanda, does one not build up hope upon the future?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, does one not build up hope upon the future.

And how, Ānanda, is one invincible in regard to presently arisen states?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, is one invincible in regard to presently arisen states.

And how, Ānanda, is one not invincible in regard to presently arisen states?...pe... Thus, Ānanda, is one not invincible in regard to presently arisen states."

"He should not pursue the past…pe…

That one is truly fortunate, the sage declares, who is at peace."

This is what the Blessed One (bhagavān) said.

The venerable Ānanda (āyasmā Ānanda), being pleased, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Ānanda-bhaddekaratta-suttaṃ, the second, is finished.
278.Sādhu sādhūti therassa sādhukāramadāsi. Sādhu kho tvanti parimaṇḍalehi padabyañjanehi parisuddhehi kathitattā desanaṃ pasaṃsanto āha. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Ānandabhaddekarattasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
278. Saying "Well done, well done! sādhu sādhū", he gave the elder an expression of approval sādhukāramadāsi. Praising the teaching desanaṃ, he said, "Well done sādhu, indeed, you tva," because it was spoken with rounded, pure phrases and expressions parimaṇḍalehi padabyañjanehi parisuddhehi kathitattā. The rest sesaṃ is clear uttānam throughout sabbattha.

The commentary on the Ānandabhaddekarattasutta is finished.
