Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s (Anāthapiṇḍada's) monastery.

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga (Pāñcakaṅga) went to the venerable Udāyī; having approached, having greeted the venerable Udāyī, he sat down at a respectful distance.

Seated at a respectful distance, the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said this to the venerable Udāyī: "How many kinds of feeling (vedanā) have been stated by the Blessed One (bhagavat)?"

"There are three feelings, householder, stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling (sukhā vedanā), painful feeling (duḥkhā vedanā), and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhā vedanā). These, householder, are the three feelings stated by the Blessed One."

When this was said, the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said this to the venerable Udāyī: "Not three feelings have been stated by the Blessed One, venerable Udāyī; two feelings have been stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling and painful feeling.

That which is neither painful nor pleasant feeling, venerable sir, that peaceful and sublime pleasure has been stated by the Blessed One."

And a second time the venerable Udāyī said this to the carpenter Pañcakaṅga: "Not two feelings have been stated by the Blessed One, householder; three feelings have been stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling, painful feeling, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. These, householder, are the three feelings stated by the Blessed One."

And a second time the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said this to the venerable Udāyī: "Not three feelings have been stated by the Blessed One, venerable Udāyī; two feelings have been stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling and painful feeling.

That which is neither painful nor pleasant feeling, venerable sir, that peaceful and sublime pleasure has been stated by the Blessed One."

And a third time the venerable Udāyī said this to the carpenter Pañcakaṅga: "Not two feelings have been stated by the Blessed One, householder; three feelings have been stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling, painful feeling, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. These, householder, are the three feelings stated by the Blessed One."

And a third time the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said this to the venerable Udāyī: "Not three feelings have been stated by the Blessed One, venerable Udāyī; two feelings have been stated by the Blessed One: pleasant feeling and painful feeling.

That which is neither painful nor pleasant feeling, venerable sir, that peaceful and sublime pleasure has been stated by the Blessed One."

The venerable Udāyī was not able to convince the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, nor was the carpenter Pañcakaṅga able to convince the venerable Udāyī.

90. “Ānanda, there are these five cords of sensual pleasure.

Which five? Forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust. Sounds cognizable by the ear… Odours cognizable by the nose…Flavours cognizable by the tongue… Tangibles cognizable by the body that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust. These are the five cords of sensual pleasure. Now the pleasure and joy that arise dependent on these five cords of sensual pleasure are called sensual pleasure (kāmasukha).

“Should anyone say: ‘That is the utmost pleasure and joy that beings experience.’ I would not concede that to him.

Why is that? Because, Ānanda, there is another kind of pleasure loftier and more sublime than that pleasure.

And what is that other kind of pleasure? 90. “Ānanda, there are these five cords of sensual pleasure (kāmaguṇā).” This is a connected account.

Not only were two kinds of feeling (vedanā) described by the Blessed One by making a beginning, but one kind of feeling was also spoken of by a method. Showing that, he began this teaching in order to support the master carpenter's statement about the five factors.

"More lovely (abhikkantatara)" means more beautiful.

"More exquisite (paṇītatara)" means more unheated.

And here, from the fourth dhyāna onward, there is a feeling of neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhā vedanā); that, too, is called pleasure in the sense of contentment and in the sense of exquisiteness.

Six kinds of domestic pleasures are called pleasure.

Cessation (nirodho), by means of pleasure without feeling (avedayitasukha), is known as bliss (sukhaṃ nāma jāto).

Indeed, by means of the five cords of sensual pleasure (pañcakāmaguṇavasena), and by means of the attainment of the eight attainments (aṭṭhasamāpattivasena), what arises is known as pleasure with feeling (vedayitasukhaṃ nāma).

Cessation (nirodho) is known as pleasure without feeling (avedayitasukhaṃ nāma).

Thus, whether it is pleasure with feeling (vedayitasukhaṃ) or pleasure without feeling (avedayitasukhaṃ), that, by the designation of a state without suffering (niddukkhabhāvasaṅkhātena), is truly known as one-sided pleasure (ekantasukhameva jātaṃ), in the sense of happiness (sukhaṭṭhena).

More lovely (abhikkantatara). More exquisite (paṇītatara).

91. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, ānanda, vijjati yaṃ aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samaṇo gotamo āha; tañca sukhasmiṃ paññapeti. Tayidaṃ kiṃsu, tayidaṃ kathaṃsū’ti? Evaṃvādino, ānanda, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā – ‘na kho, āvuso, bhagavā sukhaṃyeva vedanaṃ sandhāya sukhasmiṃ paññapeti; api ca, āvuso, yattha yattha sukhaṃ upalabbhati yahiṃ yahiṃ taṃ taṃ tathāgato sukhasmiṃ paññapetī’’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Bahuvedanīyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.
“It is possible, Ānanda, that wanderers of other sects might speak thus: ‘The recluse Gotama speaks of the cessation of perception and feeling (saññāvedayitanirodha) and he describes that as pleasure (sukha). What is this? How is this?’ Ānanda, wanderers of other sects who speak thus should be told: ‘Friends, the Blessed One (bhagavā) describes pleasure (sukha) not only with reference to pleasant feeling; rather, friends, the Tathāgata describes as pleasure any kind of pleasure wherever and in whatever way it is found.’”

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke thus.

The venerable Ānanda, being pleased, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Bahuvedanīya Sutta, the ninth, is finished.
91.Yatthayatthāti yasmiṃ yasmiṃ ṭhāne. Sukhaṃ upalabbhatīti vedayitasukhaṃ vā avedayitasukhaṃ vā upalabbhati. Taṃ taṃ tathāgato sukhasmiṃ paññapetīti taṃ sabbaṃ tathāgato niddukkhabhāvaṃ sukhasmiṃyeva paññapetīti. Idha bhagavā nirodhasamāpattiṃ sīsaṃ katvā neyyapuggalavasena arahattanikūṭeneva desanaṃ niṭṭhāpesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Bahuvedanīyasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
