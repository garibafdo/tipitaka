Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Bhūtapubbaṃ , bhikkhave, māgadhako gopālako duppaññajātiko, vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye, asamavekkhitvā gaṅgāya nadiyā orimaṃ tīraṃ, asamavekkhitvā pārimaṃ tīraṃ, atittheneva gāvo patāresi uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehānaṃ. THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Vajjian country at Ukkacela on the banks of the river Ganges.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus!”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, there was once a foolish Magadhan cowherd, who, in the last month of the rainy season, in the autumn, without examining the near shore or the further shore of the river Ganges, drove his cattle across to the other shore in the Videhan country at a place that had no ford.

Then the cattle bunched together in mid-stream in the river Ganges, and they met with calamity and disaster.

Why is that? Because that foolish Magadhan cowherd, in the last month of the rainy season, in the autumn, without examining the near shore or the further shore of the river Ganges, drove his cattle across to the other shore in the Videhan country at a place that had no ford.

Just so, monks, whichever śramaṇas or brāhmaṇas are unskilled in this world and the other world, unskilled in Māra's realm and what is outside Māra's realm, unskilled in the realm of Death and what is outside the realm of Death - it will lead to the harm and suffering for a long time of those who think they should listen to them and place faith in them.
350.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷagopālakasuttaṃ. Tattha ukkacelāyanti evaṃnāmake nagare. Tasmiṃ kira māpiyamāne rattiṃ gaṅgāsotato maccho thalaṃ patto. Manussā celāni telapātiyaṃ temetvā ukkā katvā macchaṃ gaṇhiṃsu. Nagare niṭṭhite tassa nāmaṃ karonte amhehi nagaraṭṭhānassa gahitadivase celukkāhi maccho gahitoti ukkacelā-tvevassa nāmaṃ akaṃsu. Bhikkhū āmantesīti yasmiṃ ṭhāne nisinnassa sabbā gaṅgā pākaṭā hutvā paññāyati, tādise vālikussade gaṅgātitthe sāyanhasamaye mahābhikkhusaṅghaparivuto nisīditvā mahāgaṅgaṃ paripuṇṇaṃ sandamānaṃ olokento, – ‘‘atthi nu kho imaṃ gaṅgaṃ nissāya koci pubbe vaḍḍhiparihāniṃ patto’’ti āvajjitvā, pubbe ekaṃ bālagopālakaṃ nissāya anekasatasahassā gogaṇā imissā gaṅgāya āvaṭṭe patitvā samuddameva paviṭṭhā, aparaṃ pana paṇḍitagopālakaṃ nissāya anekasatasahassagogaṇassa sotthi jātā vaḍḍhi jātā ārogyaṃ jātanti addasa. Disvā imaṃ kāraṇaṃ nissāya bhikkhūnaṃ dhammaṃ desessāmīti cintetvā bhikkhū āmantesi. Māgadhakoti magadharaṭṭhavāsī. Duppaññajātikoti nippaññasabhāvo dandho mahājaḷo. Asamavekkhitvāti asallakkhetvā anupadhāretvā. Patāresīti tāretuṃ ārabhi. Uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehānanti gaṅgāya orime tīre magadharaṭṭhaṃ, pārime tīre videharaṭṭhaṃ, gāvo magadharaṭṭhato videharaṭṭhaṃ netvā rakkhissāmīti uttaraṃ tīraṃ patāresi. Taṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ – ‘‘uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehāna’’nti. Āmaṇḍalikaṃ karitvāti maṇḍalikaṃ katvā. Anayabyasanaṃ āpajjiṃsūti avaḍḍhiṃ vināsaṃ pāpuṇiṃsu, mahāsamuddameva pavisiṃsu. Tena hi gopālakena gāvo otārentena gaṅgāya orimatīre samatitthañca visamatitthañca oloketabbaṃ assa, majjhe gaṅgāya gunnaṃ vissamaṭṭhānatthaṃ dve tīṇi vālikatthalāni sallakkhetabbāni assu. Tathā pārimatīre tīṇi cattāri titthāni, imasmā titthā bhaṭṭhā imaṃ titthaṃ gaṇhissanti, imasmā bhaṭṭhā imanti. Ayaṃ pana bālagopālako orimatīre gunnaṃ otaraṇatitthaṃ samaṃ vā visamaṃ vā anoloketvāva majjhe gaṅgāya gunnaṃ vissamaṭṭhānatthaṃ dve tīṇi vālikatthalānipi asallakkhetvāva paratīre cattāri pañca uttaraṇatitthāni asamavekkhitvāva atittheneva gāvo otāresi. Athassa mahāusabho javanasampannatāya ceva thāmasampannatāya ca tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā pārimaṃ tīraṃ patvā chinnataṭañceva kaṇṭakagumbagahanañca disvā, ‘‘dubbiniviṭṭhameta’’nti ñatvā dhuragga-patiṭṭhānokāsampi alabhitvā paṭinivatti. Gāvo mahāusabho nivatto mayampi nivattissāmāti nivattā. Mahato gogaṇassa nivattaṭṭhāne udakaṃ chijjitvā majjhe gaṅgāya āvaṭṭaṃ uṭṭhapesi. Gogaṇo āvaṭṭaṃ pavisitvā samuddameva patto. Ekopi goṇo arogo nāma nāhosi. Tenāha – ‘‘tattheva anayabyasanaṃ āpajjiṃsū’’ti. Māradheyyaṃ vuccati tebhūmakadhammā. Amāradheyyaṃ nava lokuttaradhammā. Maccudheyyampi tebhūmakadhammāva. Amaccudheyyaṃ nava lokuttaradhammā. Tattha akusalā achekā. 350. Thus, as I have heard: the Cūḷagopālaka Sūtra.

Therein, "Ukkacelā" means in a city of that name.

When it was being measured, at night a fish from the Ganges stream arrived on dry land.

People, having soaked cloths in an oil-pot, made torches and caught the fish.

When the city was finished, while they were making a name for it, they named it "Ukkacelā" because we caught a fish with cloth-torches on the day of the city's founding.

"He addressed the monks" means all the Ganges is visible in whatever place one sits; having sat down on a sandy mound on a Ganges ford of that sort at evening time, surrounded by a great assembly of monks, while gazing at the great Ganges, completely flowing, – "Indeed, having relied on this Ganges, has anyone formerly met with increase or decrease?" Having reflected, formerly, relying on one foolish cowherd, hundreds of thousands of cattle, having fallen into a whirlpool of this Ganges, entered right into the ocean. But, relying on another wise cowherd, safety arose, increase arose, health arose for hundreds of thousands of cattle.

Having seen, thinking, "Having relied on this cause, I will teach the dharma to the monks," he addressed the monks.

"Māgadhaka" means a resident of the kingdom of Magadha.

"Duppaññajātiko" means one whose nature is without wisdom, slow, greatly dull.

"Asamavekkhitvā" means without considering, without examining, without pondering.

"Patāresi" means he began to carry across.

"Uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehāna" means, "Having led the cows from the kingdom of Magadha to the kingdom of Videha on this near shore of the Ganges, on that far shore, I will protect [them]," he carried [them] to the further shore.

With reference to that, it was said – "Uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehāna."

"Āmaṇḍalikaṃ karitvā" means having made a circle.

"Anayabyasanaṃ āpajjiṃsu" means they attained decrease [and] ruin, they entered right into the great ocean.

Therefore, the ford by which the cows are carried across the Ganges, whether it is level or uneven on the near shore of the Ganges, should be looked at; in the middle of the Ganges, two or three sandy resting places for the cattle should be examined.

Likewise, on the far shore, three [or] four fords. "Having strayed from this ford, they will seize this ford; having strayed from this, this."

But this foolish cowherd, without looking at whether the ford for leading down the cows on the near shore is level or uneven, and without examining two or three sandy resting places for the cattle in the middle of the Ganges, led the cows down at a non-ford on the far shore.

Then, a great bull, by means of completeness of swiftness, and also by means of completeness of strength, having cut the stream of the Ganges crosswise, having reached the far shore, and having seen a cut bank and a thicket of thorny bushes, knowing, "This is badly situated," and also not obtaining a place for the tip of the yoke to stand, turned back.

"The cows [and] the great bull have turned back; we also will turn back," they turned back.

In the place where the great herd of cattle turned back, having cut off the water, he stirred up a whirlpool in the middle of the Ganges.

The herd of cattle, having entered the whirlpool, arrived right at the ocean.

Not even one bull was without disease.

Therefore he said – "Right there, they met with misfortune [and] calamity."

Māgadhaka, duppaññajātiko, asamavekkhitvā, patāresi, uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehāna, āmaṇḍalikaṃ karitvā, anayabyasanaṃ āpajjiṃsu.

"Akusalā imassa lokassa" means unskilled in this world, unskilled in the aggregates, elements and sense-bases, the same is true in the next world.

"Māradheyyaṃ" is said to be the three-planed dharmas.

"Amāradheyyaṃ" [are] the nine supramundane dharmas.

"Maccudheyyaṃ" also, is just the three-planed dharmas.

"Amaccudheyyaṃ" [are] the nine supramundane dharmas.

Therein, "akusalā" [means] unskilled.
Because the meaning of the term māradheyyaṃ depends on the word, it means māradheyyaṃ.

Dheyya means place, object, dwelling, or range.

The same method applies to maccudheyye.

Tesa means those śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas of such a form; by this, the teachers are shown and should be understood.

Unwholesome things are the māradheyyaṃ of this world, and amāradheyyaṃ, maccudheyya, and amaccudheyyaṃ are unwholesome and māradheyyaṃ.

Dheyya, maccudheyye, tesa.

351. ‘‘Bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhave, māgadhako gopālako sappaññajātiko, vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye, samavekkhitvā gaṅgāya nadiyā orimaṃ tīraṃ, samavekkhitvā pārimaṃ tīraṃ, tittheneva gāvo patāresi uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehānaṃ. So paṭhamaṃ patāresi ye te usabhā gopitaro gopariṇāyakā. Te tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu. Athāpare patāresi balavagāvo dammagāvo. Tepi tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu. Athāpare patāresi vacchatare vacchatariyo. Tepi tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu. Athāpare patāresi vacchake kisābalake kisabalake (sī. syā. pī.). Tepi tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu. Bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhave, vacchako taruṇako tāvadeva jātako mātugoravakena vuyhamāno, sopi tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamāsi. Taṃ kissa hetu? Tathā hi so, bhikkhave, māgadhako gopālako sappaññajātiko , vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye, samavekkhitvā gaṅgāya nadiyā orimaṃ tīraṃ, samavekkhitvā pārimaṃ tīraṃ, tittheneva gāvo patāresi uttaraṃ tīraṃ suvidehānaṃ. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye hi keci samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā kusalā imassa lokassa kusalā parassa lokassa, kusalā māradheyyassa kusalā amāradheyyassa, kusalā maccudheyyassa kusalā amaccudheyyassa, tesaṃ ye sotabbaṃ saddahātabbaṃ maññissanti, tesaṃ taṃ bhavissati dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāya.
351. Bhikkhus, there was once a wise Magadhan cowherd, in the last month of the rainy season, in the autumn, after examining the near shore and the far shore of the river Ganges (Gaṅgā), drove his cattle across to the other shore in the Videhan country at a place that had a ford.

He made the bulls, the fathers and leaders of the herd, enter first.

They breasted the stream of the Ganges (Gaṅgā) and got safely across to the further shore.

He made the strong cattle and the cattle to be tamed enter next.

They too breasted the stream of the Ganges (Gaṅgā) and got safely across to the further shore.

He made the heifers and young oxen enter next.

They too breasted the stream of the Ganges (Gaṅgā) and got safely across to the further shore.

He made the calves, the weak ones, enter next.

They too breasted the stream of the Ganges (Gaṅgā) and got safely across to the further shore.

Bhikkhus, there was once a tender calf, just born, and being urged on by its mother’s lowing, it too breasted the stream of the Ganges (Gaṅgā) and got safely across to the further shore.

Why is that? Because, bhikkhus, that wise Magadhan cowherd, in the last month of the rainy season, in the autumn, after examining the near shore and the far shore of the river Ganges (Gaṅgā), drove his cattle across to the other shore in the Videhan country at a place that had a ford.

So too, bhikkhus, as to those śramaṇas (samaṇā) and brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇā) who are skilled in this world and the other world, skilled in Māra's realm and what is outside Māra's realm, skilled in the realm of death and what is outside the realm of death - it will lead to the welfare and happiness for a long time of those who think they should listen to them and place faith in them.
351. Evaṃ kaṇhapakkhaṃ niṭṭhapetvā sukkapakkhaṃ dassento bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhavetiādimāha. Tattha balavagāvoti dantagoṇe ceva dhenuyo ca. Dammagāvoti dametabbagoṇe ceva avijātagāvo ca. Vacchatareti vacchabhāvaṃ taritvā ṭhite balavavacche. Vacchaketi dhenupake taruṇavacchake . Kisābalaketi appamaṃsalohite mandathāme. Tāvadeva jātakoti taṃdivase jātako. Mātugoravakena vuyhamānoti mātā purato purato huṃhunti goravaṃ katvā saññaṃ dadamānā urena udakaṃ chindamānā gacchati, vacchako tāya goravasaññāya dhenuyā vā urena chinnodakena gacchamāno ‘‘mātugoravakena vuyhamāno’’ti vuccati.
351. Thus, having completed the dark fortnight and showing the bright fortnight, he said, "Formerly, oh monks," and so forth.

There, "balavagāvo" means oxen with developed teeth as well as cows.

"dammagāvo" means oxen that should be tamed as well as untamed cattle.

"vacchatare" means a strong calf that has crossed beyond the state of being a calf and is established.

"vacchake" means a young calf in the care of a cow.

"kisābalake" means having little flesh and blood, with weak strength.

"tāvadeva jātako" means born on that very day.

"mātugoravakena vuyhamāno" means being carried along by honoring one's mother: the mother goes in front, making a "hum hum" sound, showing respect and giving a signal, going along cutting the water with her chest; the calf, going along following that respectful signal of the cow or the water cut by the chest, is called "being carried along by honoring one's mother."

352. ‘‘Seyyathāpi , bhikkhave, ye te usabhā gopitaro gopariṇāyakā te tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu, evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye te bhikkhū arahanto khīṇāsavā vusitavanto katakaraṇīyā ohitabhārā anuppattasadatthā parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā sammadaññā vimuttā, te tiriyaṃ mārassa sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ gatā. ‘‘Seyyathāpi te, bhikkhave, balavagāvo dammagāvo tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu, evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye te bhikkhū pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyino anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā, tepi tiriyaṃ mārassa sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ gamissanti. ‘‘Seyyathāpi te, bhikkhave, vacchatarā vacchatariyo tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu, evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye te bhikkhū tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmino sakiṃdeva imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ karissanti, tepi tiriyaṃ mārassa sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ gamissanti. ‘‘Seyyathāpi te, bhikkhave, vacchakā kisābalakā tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamaṃsu, evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye te bhikkhū tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā sotāpannā avinipātadhammā niyatā sambodhiparāyanā, tepi tiriyaṃ mārassa sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ gamissanti. ‘‘Seyyathāpi so, bhikkhave, vacchako taruṇako tāvadeva jātako mātugoravakena vuyhamāno tiriyaṃ gaṅgāya sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ agamāsi, evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye te bhikkhū dhammānusārino saddhānusārino, tepi tiriyaṃ mārassa sotaṃ chetvā sotthinā pāraṃ gamissanti. ‘‘Ahaṃ kho pana, bhikkhave, kusalo imassa lokassa kusalo parassa lokassa, kusalo māradheyyassa kusalo amāradheyyassa, kusalo maccudheyyassa kusalo amaccudheyyassa. Tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, ye sotabbaṃ saddahātabbaṃ maññissanti, tesaṃ taṃ bhavissati dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṃ vatvā sugato athāparaṃ etadavoca satthā – ‘‘Ayaṃ loko paro loko, jānatā suppakāsito; Yañca mārena sampattaṃ, appattaṃ yañca maccunā. ‘‘Sabbaṃ lokaṃ abhiññāya, sambuddhena pajānatā; Vivaṭaṃ amatadvāraṃ, khemaṃ nibbānapattiyā. ‘‘Chinnaṃ pāpimato sotaṃ, viddhastaṃ vinaḷīkataṃ; Pāmojjabahulā hotha, khemaṃ pattattha patthetha (syā. kaṃ. ka. aṭṭhakathāyaṃ saṃvaṇṇetabbapāṭho) bhikkhavo’’ti. patthetha (syā. kaṃ. ka. aṭṭhakathāyaṃ saṃvaṇṇetabbapāṭho) Cūḷagopālakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ catutthaṃ.
352. Just as the bulls, the fathers and leaders of the herd, breasted the stream of the Ganges and got safely across to the further shore, so too, those bhikkhus who are arahants with taints destroyed (khīṇāsava), who have lived the holy life, done what had to be done (katakaraṇīya), laid down the burden, reached the true goal, destroyed the fetters of being (bhava-saṃyojanā), and are completely liberated through final knowledge (sammad-aññā)- by breasting Mara’s stream they have gotten safely across to the further shore.

Just as the strong cattle and the cattle to be tamed breasted the stream of the Ganges and got safely across to the further shore, so too, those bhikkhus who, with the destruction of the five lower fetters (orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā), will reappear spontaneously (opapātikā) in the Pure Abodes and there attain final Nibbāna without ever returning from that world - by breasting Mara’s stream they will get safely across to the further shore.

Just as the heifers and young oxen breasted the stream of the Ganges and got safely across to the further shore, so too, those bhikkhus who, with the destruction of three fetters (tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā) and with the attenuation of lust (rāga), hate (dosa), and delusion (moha), are once-returners (sakadāgāmino), returning once to this world to make an end of suffering (dukkha) - by breasting Mara’s stream they too will get safely across to the further shore.

Just as the calves and the feeble cattle breasted the stream of the Ganges and got safely across to the further shore, so too, those bhikkhus who, with the destruction of three fetters (tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā), are stream-enterers (sotāpannā), no longer subject to perdition (avinipātadhammā), bound for deliverance, headed for enlightenment (sambodhi-parāyanā) - by breasting Mara’s stream they too will get safely across to the further shore.

Just as that tender calf just born, being urged on by its mother’s lowing, also breasted the stream of the Ganges and got safely across to the further shore, so too, those bhikkhus who are Dhamma-followers (dhammānusārino) and faith-followers (saddhānusārino) - by breasting Mara’s stream they too will get safely across to the further shore.

I, bhikkhus, am skilled in this world and in the other world, skilled in Mara’s realm (māradheyyassa) and in what is outside Mara’s realm (amāradheyyassa), skilled in the realm of Death (maccudheyyassa) and in what is outside the realm of Death (amaccudheyyassa).
It will lead to the welfare (hitāya) and happiness (sukhāya) for a long time of those who think they should listen to me and place faith in me.

Thus spoke the Blessed One (bhagavā).
Having said this, the Well-Gone (sugato), the Teacher (satthā) then said further:

This world, the other world, is well-proclaimed by the knowing one;
And what is reached by Māra, and what is not reached by death.

Having fully known all the world, by the fully awakened one (sambuddhena), the knowing one;
The door to the deathless (amatadvāraṃ) is opened, safety (khemaṃ) for the attainment of Nibbāna.

Cut off is the stream of the evil one (pāpimato sotaṃ), destroyed, made devoid of reeds;
Be abundant with joy (pāmojjabahulā hotha), attain safety (khemaṃ), bhikkhus.

The Lesser Cowherd Discourse (cūḷagopālakasuttaṃ), the fourth, is finished.
352.Mārassasotaṃ chetvāti arahattamaggena mārassa taṇhāsotaṃ chetvā. Pāraṃ gatāti mahāusabhā nadīpāraṃ viya saṃsārapāraṃ nibbānaṃ gatā. Pāraṃ agamaṃsūti mahāusabhānaṃ pāraṅgatakkhaṇe gaṅgāya sotassa tayo koṭṭhāse atikkamma ṭhitā mahāusabhe pāraṃ patte disvā tesaṃ gatamaggaṃ paṭipajjitvā pāraṃ agamaṃsu. Pāraṃ gamissantīti catumaggavajjhānaṃ kilesānaṃ tayo koṭṭhāse khepetvā ṭhitā idāni arahattamaggena avasesaṃ taṇhāsotaṃ chetvā balavagāvo viya nadīpāraṃ saṃsārapāraṃ nibbānaṃ gamissantīti. Iminā nayena sabbavāresu attho veditabbo. Dhammānusārino, saddhānusārinoti ime dve paṭhamamaggasamaṅgino. Jānatāti sabbadhamme jānantena buddhena. Suppakāsitoti sukathito. Vivaṭanti vivaritaṃ. Amatadvāranti ariyamaggo. Nibbānapattiyāti tadatthāya vivaṭaṃ. Vinaḷīkatanti vigatamānanaḷaṃ kataṃ. Khemaṃ patthethāti kattukamyatāchandena arahattaṃ patthetha, kattukāmā nibbattetukāmā hothāti attho. ‘‘Patta’tthā’’tipi pāṭho. Evarūpaṃ satthāraṃ labhitvā tumhe pattāyeva nāmāti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Bhagavā pana yathānusandhināva desanaṃ niṭṭhapesīti. Jānatā Suppakāsito Vivaṭa Amatadvāra Nibbānapattiyā Vinaḷīkata Khemaṃ patthethā Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷagopālakasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
352.

Mārassa sotaṃ chetvā means having severed the stream of Māra, severing the stream of craving ( taṇhā) of Māra by the path of the Arhat ( arahattamaggena).

Pāraṃ gatā means having gone to the further shore of saṃsāra, like Mahāusabha having gone to the further shore of the river, [they] have gone to nirvāṇa.

Pāraṃ agamaṃsū means those who, at the moment Mahāusabha went to the further shore (pāraṅgatakkhaṇe), stood having crossed the three portions of the stream of the Ganges (gaṅgāya sotassa tayo koṭṭhāse atikkamma ṭhitā), seeing Mahāusabha having reached the further shore (mahāusabhe pāraṃ patte disvā), having followed the path he took (tesaṃ gatamaggaṃ paṭipajjitvā), they went to the further shore (pāraṃ agamaṃsu).

Pāraṃ gamissantī means those who, of the four paths (catumaggavajjhānaṃ), having destroyed the three portions of the defilements (kilesānaṃ tayo koṭṭhāse khepetvā ṭhitā), now, by the path of the Arhat (arahattamaggena), having severed the remaining stream of craving (avasesaṃ taṇhāsotaṃ chetvā), like strong cattle (balavagāvo viya), will go to the further shore of the river, to the further shore of saṃsāra, to nirvāṇa (nadīpāraṃ saṃsārapāraṃ nibbānaṃ gamissantīti).

In this way (iminā nayena), the meaning (attho) should be understood in all instances (sabbavāresu veditabbo).

Dhammānusārino, saddhānusārino means these two (ime dve) are complete with the first path (paṭhamamaggasamaṅgino).

Jānatā means by the Buddha, who knows all things (sabbadhamme jānantena buddhena).

Suppakāsito means well-spoken (sukathito).

Vivaṭa means opened (vivaritaṃ).

Amatadvāra means the noble path ( ariyamaggo).

Nibbānapattiyā means opened for that purpose (tadatthāya vivaṭaṃ).

Vinaḷīkata means vigatamānanaḷaṃ kataṃ.

Khemaṃ patthethā means you should aspire to Arhatship (arahattaṃ patthetha) with the desire to do (kattukamyatāchandena), the desire to produce, the desire to be (kattukāmā nibbattetukāmā hothāti attho).

There is also the reading, "attained meaning" (patta'tthā'tipi pāṭho).

Having obtained such a teacher ( evarūpaṃ satthāraṃ labhitvā), you have indeed attained [it] (tumhe pattāyeva nāmāti attho).

The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva).

The Blessed One (bhagavā), indeed, completed the teaching with accordance (yathānusandhināva desanaṃ niṭṭhapesīti).

Jānatā suppakāsito vivaṭa amatadvāra nibbānapattiyā vinaḷīkata khemaṃ patthethā

The Cūḷagopālakasuttavaṇṇanā, [a commentary] of the Majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathā, [belonging to] the Papañcasūdanī, is finished.
