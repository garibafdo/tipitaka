434. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ yena nagaravindaṃ nāma kosalānaṃ brāhmaṇānaṃ gāmo tadavasari. Assosuṃ kho nagaravindeyyakā nagaravindeyyā (ka.) brāhmaṇagahapatikā – ‘‘samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ nagaravindaṃ anuppatto. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti. Atha kho nagaravindeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā appekacce bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu; sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavato santike nāmagottaṃ sāvetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce tuṇhībhūtā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinne kho nagaravindeyyake brāhmaṇagahapatike bhagavā etadavoca –
434. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kosalan country with a large saṃgha of monks, and eventually arrived at a Kosalan village named Nagaravinda, inhabited by brāhmaṇas.

The brāhmaṇa householders of Nagaravinda heard: “The śramaṇa Gotama, the son of the Śākyans, who went forth from the Śākya clan, while wandering in the Kosalan country with a large saṃgha of monks, has arrived at Nagaravinda.”

Now a good report of that Venerable Gotama has spread about: “That Blessed One is an arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha, perfect in knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), a Well-Farer (sugata), Knower of the Worlds (lokavidū), unsurpassed Trainer of those who can be led, Teacher of gods and humans, a Buddha, a Blessed One.”

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge (abhijñā), proclaims this world with its gods (sadevakaṃ), its Māras (samārakaṃ) and Brahmās (sabrahmakaṃ), its ascetics and brāhmaṇas (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ), its princes and people (sadevamanussaṃ).

He teaches the Dharma that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with the (right) meaning and the (right) words; he reveals the spiritual life (brahmacariyaṃ) that is utterly perfect and pure.

“It is good to see such arhats.”

Then the brāhmaṇa householders of Nagaravinda went to the Blessed One. Some paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some exchanged greetings with him, and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, sat down at one side; some extended their hands in reverential salutation (añjaliṃ paṇāmetvā) towards the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some pronounced their name and clan in the Blessed One’s presence and sat down at one side; some kept silent and sat down at one side.

When the brāhmaṇa householders of Nagaravinda were seated to one side, the Blessed One said this to them:

435. ‘‘Sace vo, gahapatayo, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘kathaṃbhūtā, gahapatayo, samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā’ti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha – ‘ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari apassataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā sotaviññeyyesu saddesu… ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu… jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu… kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu… manoviññeyyesu dhammesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi manoviññeyyesu dhammesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā , ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari apassataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā’ti. Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha.
435. "If, householders, wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: 'What kind of recluses and Brahmins should not be honored, respected, revered, and venerated?' Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects thus: 'Those recluses and Brahmins who are not rid of lust ( avītarāga), hate ( avītadosa), and delusion ( avītamoha) regarding forms cognizable by the eye ( cakṣuviññeyyesu rūpesu), whose minds are not inwardly peaceful ( ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and who conduct themselves now righteously, now unrighteously ( samavisamaṃ caranti) in body ( kāyena), speech ( vācāya), and mind ( manasā) - such recluses and Brahmins should not be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.'

What is the reason for this? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust ( avītarāga), hate ( avītadosa), and delusion ( avītamoha) regarding forms cognizable by the eye ( cakṣuviññeyyesu rūpesu), our minds are not inwardly peaceful ( ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously ( samavisamaṃ carāma) in body ( kāyena), speech ( vācāya), and mind ( manasā). Since we do not see any higher righteous conduct on the part of those, they should not be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.

Those recluses and Brahmins who are not rid of lust ( avītarāga), hate ( avītadosa), and delusion ( avītamoha) regarding sounds cognizable by the ear ( sotaviññeyyesu saddesu)...regarding odours cognizable by the nose ( ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu)...regarding flavours cognizable by the tongue ( jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu)...regarding tangibles cognizable by the body ( kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu)...regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind ( manoviññeyyesu dhammesu), whose minds are not inwardly peaceful ( ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and who conduct themselves now righteously, now unrighteously ( samavisamaṃ caranti) in body ( kāyena), speech ( vācāya), and mind ( manasā) - such recluses and Brahmins should not be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.

What is the reason for this? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust ( avītarāga), hate ( avītadosa), and delusion ( avītamoha) regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind ( manoviññeyyesu dhammesu), our minds are not inwardly peaceful ( ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously ( samavisamaṃ carāma) in body ( kāyena), speech ( vācāya), and mind ( manasā). Since we do not see any higher righteous conduct on the part of those, they should not be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.’

Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way.

436. ‘‘Sace pana vo, gahapatayo, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘kathaṃbhūtā, gahapatayo, samaṇabrāhmaṇā sakkātabbā garukātabbā mānetabbā pūjetabbā’ti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha – ‘ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu vītarāgā vītadosā vītamohā, ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacittā, samacariyaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā sakkātabbā garukātabbā mānetabbā pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi mayaṃ hi (?) cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari passataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā sakkātabbā garukātabbā mānetabbā pūjetabbā. Ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā sotaviññeyyesu saddesu… ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu… jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu… kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu… manoviññeyyesu dhammesu vītarāgā vītadosā vītamohā, ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacittā, samacariyaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā sakkātabbā garukātabbā mānetabbā pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi manoviññeyyesu dhammesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari passataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā sakkātabbā garukātabbā mānetabbā pūjetabbā’ti . Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha.
436. “If, householders, wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: ‘What kind of ascetics and brāhmaṇas, householders, should be honored, respected, revered, and venerated?’ Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects thus: ‘Those ascetics and brāhmaṇas who are rid of lust ( vītarāga), hate (vītadosa), and delusion (vītamoha) regarding forms cognizable by the eye ( cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu), whose minds are inwardly peaceful (ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacittā), and who conduct themselves righteously ( samacariyaṃ caranti) in body ( kāyena), speech ( vācāya), and mind (manasā) - such ascetics and brāhmaṇas should be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.’

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust (avītarāga), hate (avītadosa), and delusion (avītamoha) regarding forms cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu), our minds are not inwardly peaceful (ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously (samavisamaṃ carāma) in body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā). We do not see higher righteous conduct on their part.

Therefore, those good ascetics and brāhmaṇas should be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.

Those ascetics and brāhmaṇas who are rid of lust (vītarāgā), hate (vītadosā), and delusion (vītamohā) regarding sounds cognizable by the ear (sotaviññeyyesu saddesu)… regarding odours cognizable by the nose (ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu)… regarding flavours cognizable by the tongue (jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu)… regarding tangibles cognizable by the body (kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu)… regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind (manoviññeyyesu dhammesu), whose minds are inwardly peaceful (ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacittā), and who conduct themselves righteously (samacariyaṃ caranti) in body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā) - such ascetics and brāhmaṇas should be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust (avītarāgā), hate (avītadosā), and delusion (avītamohā) regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind (manoviññeyyesu dhammesu), our minds are not inwardly peaceful (ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā), and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously (samavisamaṃ carāma) in body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā). We do not see higher righteous conduct on their part.

Therefore, those good ascetics and brāhmaṇas should be honored, respected, revered, and venerated.’

Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way.

437. ‘‘Sace pana vo sace te (syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.), gahapatayo, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘ke panāyasmantānaṃ ākārā, ke anvayā, yena tumhe āyasmanto evaṃ vadetha? Addhā te āyasmanto vītarāgā vā rāgavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītadosā vā dosavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītamohā vā mohavinayāya vā paṭipannā’ti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha – ‘tathā hi te āyasmanto araññavanapatthāni pantāni senāsanāni paṭisevanti. Natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā ye disvā disvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā sotaviññeyyā saddā ye sutvā sutvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā ghānaviññeyyā gandhā ye ghāyitvā ghāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ , natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā jivhāviññeyyā rasā ye sāyitvā sāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā ye phusitvā phusitvā abhirameyyuṃ. Ime kho no, āvuso, ākārā, ime anvayā, yena mayaṃ yena mayaṃ āyasmanto (sī. pī.), yena mayaṃ āyasmante (syā. kaṃ.) evaṃ vadema – addhā te āyasmanto vītarāgā vā rāgavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītadosā vā dosavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītamohā vā mohavinayāya vā paṭipannā’ti. Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyāthā’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, nagaravindeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama! Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti; evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Ete mayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāma dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsake no bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupete saraṇaṃ gate’’ti. Nagaravindeyyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.

"If, householders, wanderers belonging to other sects should ask you thus: 'What are the reasons, what is the evidence whereby you venerable ones speak thus? Surely these venerable ones are either rid of lust ( vītarāga ) or are practising for the removal of lust ( rāgavinayāya ), either rid of hatred ( vītadosa ) or are practicing for the removal of hatred ( dosavinayāya ), either rid of delusion ( vītamoha ) or are practising for the removal of delusion ( mohavinayāya )?' Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way: 'The venerable ones frequent secluded lodgings in the forests and woods.
For there are no forms cognizable by the eye there of a kind that they could look at and delight in ( cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā ye disvā disvā abhirameyyuṃ ), there are no sounds cognizable by the ear there of a kind that they could listen to and delight in ( sotaviññeyyā saddā ye sutvā sutvā abhirameyyuṃ ), there are no odours cognizable by the nose there of a kind that they could smell and delight in ( ghānaviññeyyā gandhā ye ghāyitvā ghāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ ) , there are no flavours cognizable by the tongue there of a kind that they could taste and delight in ( jivhāviññeyyā rasā ye sāyitvā sāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ ), there are no tangibles cognizable by the body there of a kind that they could touch and delight in ( kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā ye phusitvā phusitvā abhirameyyuṃ ).
These, friends, are our reasons, this is our evidence ( ime ākārā, ime anvayā ) whereby we say: 'Surely these venerable ones are either rid of lust ( vītarāga ) or are practising for the removal of lust ( rāgavinayāya ), either rid of hatred ( vītadosa ) or are practicing for the removal of hatred ( dosavinayāya ), either rid of delusion ( vītamoha ) or are practising for the removal of delusion ( mohavinayāya ).'
Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way.'"
When this was said, the brahmin householders of Nagaravindeyya said to the Blessed One: "Magnificent, Master Gotama! Magnificent, Master Gotama! Just as, Master Gotama, one might turn upright what had been overthrown, reveal what was hidden, show the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark for those with eyesight to see forms, so the Dhamma has been made clear in many ways by Master Gotama.
We go to Master Gotama for refuge ( saraṇaṃ gacchāma ), to the Dhamma, and to the Sangha of monks ( bhikkhusaṅghañca ).
May Master Gotama remember us as lay followers who have gone to him for refuge ( upāsake saraṇaṃ gate ), from this day forward, for life."
The Nagaravindeyya Sutta, the eighth, is finished.
437.Ke ākārāti kāni kāraṇāni? Ke anvayāti kā anubuddhiyo? Natthi kho pana tatthāti kasmā āha, nanu araññe haritatiṇacampakavanādivasena atimanuññā rūpādayo pañca kāmaguṇā atthīti? No natthi. Na panetaṃ vanasaṇḍena kathitaṃ, itthirūpādīni pana sandhāyetaṃ kathitaṃ. Tāni hi purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti. Yathāha – ‘‘nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekarūpampi samanupassāmi, yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati, yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthirūpaṃ. Itthirūpaṃ, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī’’ti (a. ni. 1.1) vitthāretabbaṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Nagaravindeyyasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
437. "What are the forms (ākārā)?" Namely, what are the causes (kāraṇāni)? "What are the connections (anvayā)?" Namely, what are the understandings (anubuddhiyo)? "But there is nothing there (natthi kho pana tatthā)": Why does he say this? Surely, in the forest (araññe), there are very attractive (atimanuññā) sights, sounds, etc. (rūpādayo), the five sense pleasures (pañca kāmaguṇā), are there not? No, there are not.

But this was not said about the forest grove (vanasaṇḍena). But this was said with reference to the forms of women, and so on (itthirūpādīni).

For they (tāni) invade (pariyādāya) and remain (tiṭṭhanti) in a man's mind (purisassa cittaṃ).

As he said: “Monks (bhikkhave), I do not see any other form (aññaṃ ekarūpampi) that so invades (pariyādāya) and remains (tiṭṭhati) in a man's mind (purisassa cittaṃ) as this, monks (bhikkhave), the form of a woman (itthirūpaṃ).

The form of a woman (itthirūpaṃ), monks (bhikkhave), invades (pariyādāya) and remains (tiṭṭhati) in a man's mind (purisassa cittaṃ),” etc. (A.
